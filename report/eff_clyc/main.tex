\begin{abstract}
This report compares simulated and measured full-energy peak efficiencies for the CLYC2 detector at nominal source-to-detector distances of \SI{200}{mm}, \SI{300}{mm}, and \SI{400}{mm}. The simulated efficiency is obtained from isotropic Geant4 runs with $10^7$ gammas per point and a $\pm\SI{2}{\percent}$ total-energy window. The measured efficiency is obtained from the 2026-10-27 CLYC2 spectra after subtracting the \SI{600}{s} environmental background and fitting each peak with a Gaussian plus linear background over $\pm1.5$ FWHM. No external efficiency table is used for the distance comparison. At \SI{661.64}{keV} the simulation/measurement ratio is 0.98--1.03; at \SI{1332.51}{keV} it is 0.92--1.11. At \SI{1173.23}{keV} the simulation is 19--36\% larger than the measurement. The \SI{1173.23}{keV} peak sits on the Compton continuum from the \SI{1332.51}{keV} line; the Gaussian peak area has a \SI{10}{\percent} background-model uncertainty, but the excess is larger. The measured \SI{1173}{keV}/\SI{1332}{keV} ratio is 1.00--1.04, compared with 1.18 in the simulation. Cascade summing is negligible at these distances, so the line-dependent excess points to the Co-60 source geometry or the detector response model. Separately, the \SI{2}{cm} calibration file is compared with a radioactive-decay simulation in which the source puck is flush with the detector front face. The flush simulation agrees at \SI{46.54}{keV}, \SI{661.64}{keV}, and \SI{1408.01}{keV} and, after pulse clustering, at \SI{59.54}{keV}; the \SI{121.78}{keV}, \SI{344.28}{keV}, and \ce{^{60}Co} lines remain high. The detector resolution is $R(E)=(123.9\pm0.4)/\sqrt{E/\mathrm{keV}}\,\%$ (FWHM $=1.239\sqrt{E/\mathrm{keV}}\,\mathrm{keV}$), fitted to the \SI{661.64}{keV} and \SI{1332.51}{keV} lines.
\end{abstract}

\section{Geometry and Solid Angle Efficiency Bound}

The detector model uses a cylindrical CLYC crystal of diameter \SI{25}{mm} and length \SI{25}{mm} (radius $r=\SI{12.5}{mm}$, density \SI{3.31}{g\,cm^{-3}}) placed inside a stepped aluminium casing (\texttt{GeometryCLYC.cc}). The front face of the crystal sits \SI{2}{mm} behind the front surface of the detector casing ($z_{\mathrm{recess}}=\SI{2}{mm}$), consisting of a \SI{0.5}{mm} aluminium entrance window and a \SI{1.5}{mm} Teflon reflector cap. Behind the \SI{29}{mm} snout, the wider detector body and PMT are surrounded by a \SI{0.64}{mm}-thick MuMetal magnetic shield. The calibration sources are modeled as isotropic point sources at the centre of an epoxy puck of radius \SI{10}{mm}, thickness \SI{2}{mm}, and density \SI{1.16}{g\,cm^{-3}} (\ce{H32C15N2O4}).

For a nominal source-to-detector distance $d_{\mathrm{nom}}$, the true distance between the source and the crystal front face is $d=d_{\mathrm{nom}}+\SI{2}{mm}$. Figure~\ref{fig:setup} shows the axial geometry and the solid-angle cone.

\subsection{Solid angle and geometric acceptance}
For an isotropic point source emitting into $4\pi$, the solid angle subtended by the circular crystal entrance face of radius $r$ at distance $d$ along the detector axis is
\begin{equation}
  \Omega(d)=2\pi\left(1-\frac{d}{\sqrt{d^2+r^2}}\right),
  \label{eq:solid-angle}
\end{equation}
and the geometric acceptance is
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon_\Omega(d)=\frac{\Omega(d)}{4\pi}
  =\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{d}{\sqrt{d^2+r^2}}\right)\times100\%.
  \label{eq:omega}
\end{equation}
For the geometries used here,
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon_\Omega =
  \begin{cases}
    \SI{0.0955}{\percent}, & d_{\mathrm{nom}} = \SI{200}{mm} \ (d=\SI{202}{mm}),\\
    \SI{0.0428}{\percent}, & d_{\mathrm{nom}} = \SI{300}{mm} \ (d=\SI{302}{mm}),\\
    \SI{0.0242}{\percent}, & d_{\mathrm{nom}} = \SI{400}{mm} \ (d=\SI{402}{mm}),\\
    \SI{38.33}{\percent}, & d_{\mathrm{nom}} = \SI{1}{mm} \ (d=\SI{3}{mm})\quad\text{(flush)}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:omega-values}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Maximum efficiency bound and intrinsic efficiency}
The total full-energy peak efficiency factorizes as
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon=\epsilon_\Omega(d)\,\epsilon_{\mathrm{int}}(E_\gamma,d),
  \label{eq:factorization}
\end{equation}
where $\epsilon_{\mathrm{int}}\in[0,1]$ is the probability that a gamma entering the crystal deposits all of its energy. Because $\epsilon_{\mathrm{int}}\le1$, the geometric acceptance is a hard upper bound,
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon\le\epsilon_{\max}(d)=\epsilon_\Omega(d).
  \label{eq:eff-max}
\end{equation}

\section{Calculation Methods}

\subsection{Measured efficiency}
The 2026-10-27 CLYC2 spectra are converted from CNF to ROOT with \texttt{cnf2root}. The converter writes the channel-count histogram, the calibrated energy histogram, and the acquisition metadata, including live time and the energy calibration. The analysis uses the native $\sim\SI{1.96}{keV}$ energy bins; rebinning to \SI{2}{keV} by bin centre would alias the two grids.

The \SI{600}{s} environmental background spectrum is subtracted bin by bin. This removes room and cosmic background; it does not remove source-induced Compton continuum. Each full-energy peak is then fitted with a Gaussian plus linear background over $\pm1.5$ FWHM around the observed peak. The fit range for the \SI{1173.23}{keV} line is \SI{1113.5}{keV}--\SI{1233.0}{keV}; for the \SI{1332.51}{keV} line it is \SI{1264.4}{keV}--\SI{1400.6}{keV}. The Gaussian area is the measured peak area.

The measured efficiency is
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon_{\mathrm{exp}}=\frac{N_{\mathrm{peak}}}{A_{\mathrm{exact}}P_\gamma t_{\mathrm{live}}/100}\times100\%,
  \label{eq:eff-exp}
\end{equation}
where $A_{\mathrm{exact}}$ is the source activity at the measurement date, $P_\gamma$ is the gamma-ray emission probability in percent, $t_{\mathrm{live}}=\SI{600}{s}$, and $N_{\mathrm{peak}}$ is the Gaussian peak area. The statistical uncertainty is obtained from the Gaussian fit.

For the \SI{1173.23}{keV} line, the peak sits on the Compton continuum from the \SI{1332.51}{keV} line. A local sideband integration over the $\pm1$ FWHM peak region with sidebands at 1.2--1.8 FWHM gives a peak area that differs from the Gaussian-plus-linear fit by about \SI{10}{\percent}. This is treated as the background-model systematic for the \SI{1173.23}{keV} line. For the \SI{661.64}{keV} and \SI{1332.51}{keV} lines the two methods agree to about \SI{1}{\percent}.

\subsection{Simulated efficiency}
The simulations use \texttt{G4EmStandardPhysics\_option4}. For each energy and distance, $10^7$ monoenergetic gammas are generated isotropically from the puck centre. All energy deposited in the CLYC crystal is summed per event. Full-energy peak events satisfy
\begin{equation}
  0.98E_\gamma\le E_{\mathrm{dep}}\le1.02E_\gamma.
  \label{eq:sim-window}
\end{equation}
The simulated efficiency and statistical uncertainty are
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon_{\mathrm{sim}}=\frac{N_{\mathrm{peak}}}{N_{\mathrm{inc}}}\times100\%,
  \qquad
  \sigma_{\mathrm{sim}}=\frac{\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{peak}}}}{N_{\mathrm{inc}}}\times100\%,
  \label{eq:eff-sim}
\end{equation}
with $N_{\mathrm{inc}}=10^7$. The simulation is not resolution-smeared here; the full-energy peak is a delta line, so the $\pm2\%$ window contains the complete peak.

The simulation/measurement ratio and statistical pull are
\begin{equation}
  R=\frac{\epsilon_{\mathrm{sim}}}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{exp}}},
  \qquad
  \mathrm{pull}=\frac{\epsilon_{\mathrm{sim}}-\epsilon_{\mathrm{exp}}}
  {\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathrm{sim}}^2+\sigma_{\mathrm{exp}}^2}}.
  \label{eq:ratio-pull}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Flush source response}
For the flush geometry, the source puck touches the detector front face. The
source is at the puck centre, \SI{1}{mm} from the detector front plane and
\SI{3}{mm} from the crystal front face. The decay chain is tracked with
\texttt{G4RadioactiveDecayPhysics} using parent ions at rest at the puck centre
($N_{\mathrm{decays}}=10^6$ per source). The long-decay threshold is
\SI{1e60}{y}, and the analysis merges pulses whose leading-edge times differ by
at most the \SI{1}{\micro\second} CLYC resolving time. The \SI{1e4}{y}
cross-check stops the \ce{^{241}Am} chain at \ce{^{237}Np}; it does not change
the \ce{^{137}Cs}, \ce{^{210}Pb}, \ce{^{152}Eu}, or \ce{^{60}Co} results.
The simulated peak efficiency is
\begin{equation}
  \epsilon_{\mathrm{sim}}(E_\gamma)=
  \frac{N_{\mathrm{pulse}}(E_\gamma)}{N_{\mathrm{decays}}P_\gamma(E_\gamma)}
  \times100\%,
  \label{eq:flush-eff}
\end{equation}
with the statistical uncertainty obtained from the pulse count.

\subsection{The \SI{2}{cm} calibration file}
The calibration file \texttt{eff\_clyc1.csv} is labelled as a \SI{2}{cm}
measurement. The file has no detector column and is treated as CLYC1-only.
Its printed values match Eq.~\eqref{eq:eff-exp} without a factor of
\num{1000}. At lower energies (\SI{32}{keV}--\SI{121}{keV}) the values range
from \SI{15.4}{\percent} to \SI{23.9}{\percent}. The file documents neither the
actual source position nor the detector identity, so the measured values are
compared with the flush simulation of Sec.~\ref{sec:source_response}.

The \ce{^{137}Cs} \SI{661.64}{keV} ratio is 0.98--1.03 at the three distances, with pulls between $-0.4$ and $+0.8\sigma$. The \ce{^{60}Co} \SI{1332.51}{keV} ratio is 0.92--1.11, with pulls between $-0.8$ and $+1.8\sigma$. The \SI{1173.23}{keV} ratio is 1.19--1.36, with pulls between $+2.0$ and $+4.4\sigma$.

The \SI{1173.23}{keV} line is therefore the outlier. The measured \SI{1173}{keV}/\SI{1332}{keV} ratio is 1.00--1.04, while the simulated ratio is 1.18. The \SI{1173.23}{keV} Gaussian area has the \SI{10}{\percent} background-model systematic from the sideband check; the \SI{10}{\percent} background-model uncertainty does not remove the excess. The \SI{1332.51}{keV} line changes by only 0.92--1.11, so the excess is line-dependent rather than a common normalization.

At \SI{200}{mm}--\SI{400}{mm} the Co-60 cascade summing is negligible: the total interaction probability per gamma is about $3.3\times10^{-4}$, so the probability that both cascade gammas interact is about $10^{-7}$ and the \SI{2505.74}{keV} sum peak is negligible. The \SI{1173.23}{keV} excess is therefore not cascade summing. It points to an energy-dependent source or detector effect, such as the actual Co-60 source capsule or holder, the detector active volume, or the reflector/casing model. The available data do not identify which.

\section{Experimental Spectra and Peak Integration}
\label{sec:peak_integration}

The 2026-10-27 CLYC2 spectra are the only measured spectra used in this report. The CNF conversion, \SI{600}{s} background subtraction, and Gaussian fits are described in Sec.~\ref{sec:distance_results}. The Gaussian peak areas for the nine points are the measured efficiencies in Table~\ref{tab:comparison}. The fit ranges are $\pm1.5$ FWHM; the \SI{1173.23}{keV} fit range is \SI{1113.5}{keV}--\SI{1233.0}{keV}. The local sideband integration over the same peak region gives an independent area; for \SI{1173.23}{keV} it differs from the Gaussian fit by about \SI{10}{\percent}, and this is the continuum-model systematic. For \SI{661.64}{keV} and \SI{1332.51}{keV} the two methods agree to about \SI{1}{\percent}.

Figure~\ref{fig:exp_sim_co_fits} shows the Gaussian-plus-quadratic-background fits to the 200 mm Co-60 spectra. This figure is a diagnostic: the table uses the Gaussian-plus-linear fit for the measured efficiency, while the figure uses a quadratic background because the \SI{1173.23}{keV} peak sits on the curved \SI{1332.51}{keV} Compton continuum. The fitted simulation/experiment peak-area ratios in the figure are 1.21 at \SI{1173.23}{keV} and 1.04 at \SI{1332.51}{keV}; the table gives 1.30 and 1.11. The difference between the two ratios is the background-model uncertainty of the \SI{1173.23}{keV} line.

\section{Detector Resolution and Simulated Spectrum Comparison}
\label{sec:resolution}

\subsection{Resolution model}
The FWHM of the \SI{661.64}{keV}, \SI{1173.23}{keV}, and \SI{1332.51}{keV} peaks is extracted from the same Gaussian-plus-linear fits at 200, 300, and 400 mm. The three distances give consistent widths; Table~\ref{tab:resolution} lists the mean and the standard error of the mean. The resolution \(R=\mathrm{FWHM}/E\) is \SI{4.80}{\percent} at \SI{661.64}{keV}, \SI{3.40}{\percent} at \SI{1173.23}{keV}, and \SI{3.41}{\percent} at \SI{1332.51}{keV}. The \SI{1173.23}{keV} width is affected by the broad background under the peak, so it is not used in the fit. The two stable lines give
\[
  \mathrm{FWHM}(E)=\SI{1.239}{\sqrt{E/\mathrm{keV}}}\,\SI{}{\kilo\electronvolt},
  \qquad
  R(E)=\frac{123.9\pm0.4}{\sqrt{E/\mathrm{keV}}}\,\%.
\]
The \SI{1173.23}{keV} point lies \SI{6.2}{\percent} below this curve, consistent with the background-model bias.

The model is used only to smear the simulated spectra: each event with deposited energy \(E_{\mathrm{dep}}\) is replaced by a Gaussian draw with mean \(E_{\mathrm{dep}}\) and standard deviation \(\sigma(E_{\mathrm{dep}})=\mathrm{FWHM}(E_{\mathrm{dep}})/2.3548\). The smearing conserves the number of events and does not change the peak areas. The model is an extrapolation between and below the fitted lines.

\subsection{Simulated and experimental spectra at 200 mm}
\label{sec:spectra_200mm}

The 200 mm simulated spectra use the existing $10^7$-event monoenergetic runs, with the GPS emission restricted to a 15 degree cone around the detector axis. The cone increases the number of events that reach the crystal by the inverse of the cone solid-angle fraction $f=(1-\cos15^\circ)/2=0.017037$; the equivalent isotropic incident count is $N_{\mathrm{inc,eff}}=N_{\mathrm{gen}}/f=\num{5.87e8}$. The cone run reproduces the isotropic peak efficiencies within about \(1\sigma\): \SI{0.00871}{\percent} versus \SI{0.00843}{\percent} at \SI{661.64}{keV}, \SI{0.00373}{\percent} versus \SI{0.00384}{\percent} at \SI{1173.23}{keV}, and \SI{0.00308}{\percent} versus \SI{0.00326}{\percent} at \SI{1332.51}{keV}. The cone cut removes gammas emitted outside 15 degrees that scatter into the crystal; those gammas contribute mainly to the continuum, so the peak comparison is quantitative and the continuum comparison is qualitative.

Both simulated spectra (with and without resolution) use the same events and the same scaling. The number of emitted gammas is
\[
  N_{\mathrm{emit}}=A_{\mathrm{exact}}P_\gamma t_{\mathrm{live}}/100,
\]
and the scale factor is $N_{\mathrm{emit}}f/N_{\mathrm{gen}}$. For Cs-137 at 200 mm, $N_{\mathrm{emit}}=\num{2.834e8}$ and the scale factor is 0.4828. For Co-60, the \SI{1173.23}{keV} and \SI{1332.51}{keV} runs are summed, each with its own $N_{\mathrm{emit}}$ (\num{7.031e8} and \num{7.032e8}) and scale factor 1.198. The unsmeared spectrum fills each event at its deposited energy; the smeared spectrum replaces it by a Gaussian draw with $\sigma(E_{\mathrm{dep}})=\mathrm{FWHM}(E_{\mathrm{dep}})/2.3548$. The \SI{2505.74}{keV} sum peak is not present in the monoenergetic runs.

For Cs-137 at 200 mm, the resolution-smeared simulation reproduces the \SI{661.64}{keV} peak: the ratio of the background-subtracted counts to the smeared simulation in the \(\pm2\%\) interval is 0.955, and the smeared simulated peak area is about \SI{5}{\percent} above the measured Gaussian area. The simulation lies below the measured continuum at low energy; the experimental spectrum contains room background and scattered radiation from the source environment, which the monoenergetic simulation does not. The \SI{200}{mm} Cs-137 spectrum has no resolved \SI{32}{keV} Ba X-ray peak, so the source encapsulation may absorb the X-rays; the simulation does not model the encapsulation. The \SI{661.64}{keV} peak itself is the quantitative comparison.

For Co-60 at 200 mm, the table gives $\epsilon_{\mathrm{sim}}/\epsilon_{\mathrm{exp}}=1.30$ at \SI{1173.23}{keV} and 1.11 at \SI{1332.51}{keV}. The bin-by-bin ratio in Fig.~\ref{fig:exp_sim_co}(b) is not the same quantity. The \(\pm2\%\) interval at \SI{1173.23}{keV} is \SI{46.9}{keV} wide, or \num{1.18} FWHM, and contains Compton continuum from the \SI{1332.51}{keV} line: the background-subtracted interval integral is \num{23396} counts, compared with \num{20800} in the Gaussian peak, while the smeared simulation gives \num{24306} counts in the interval and about \num{26200} in the full-energy peak. The interval ratio is therefore \num{0.963}, whereas the full-energy peak ratio is $20{,}819/26{,}210=0.79$ (simulation/measurement \num{1.26}), consistent with the table ratio 1.30. Figure~\ref{fig:exp_sim_co_fits} shows the fitted peaks and their Gaussian and background components; the fitted ratios are 1.21 and 1.04. The \SI{1173.23}{keV} peak area has the \SI{10}{\percent} background-model uncertainty from Sec.~\ref{sec:peak_integration}; the excess is larger. The \SI{1332.51}{keV} agreement is within the peak-integral uncertainty. The experimental Co-60 spectrum has lines at \SI{141.8}{keV} and \SI{222.1}{keV} that are not Co-60 lines and are absent from the simulation. The \SI{2505.74}{keV} sum peak is also absent; at \SI{200}{mm} it is expected to be small, about 5 counts, so it does not explain the \SI{1173.23}{keV} excess. At 200--400 mm the total interaction probability per gamma is about \(3.3\times10^{-4}\), so the summing-out probability is below \SI{0.05}{\percent}. The Co-60 excess is therefore not cascade summing; the Co-60 source geometry or the detector response model is the more likely cause.

The flush simulation is physically consistent throughout: it stays below the
\SI{38.33}{\percent} geometric bound at every line, and the \SI{32}{keV} peak
efficiency (\SI{15.90}{\percent}) is lower than the \SI{46.54}{keV} and
\SI{59.54}{keV} values because photoelectric absorption in the \SI{2}{mm}
epoxy puck attenuates the \SI{32}{keV} X-ray. Above \SI{122}{keV}, the
efficiency drops smoothly with energy to \SI{0.431}{\percent} at
\SI{1408}{keV}.

A puck touching the detector front face puts the source \SI{1}{mm} from the
front plane and \SI{3}{mm} from the crystal. The puck is \SI{2}{mm} thick and
the source is at its centre; the crystal front face is \SI{2}{mm} behind the
front plane. Eq.~\eqref{eq:omega} then gives
\(\epsilon_\Omega(3\,\mathrm{mm})=\SI{38.33}{\percent}\). Figure~\ref{fig:source_eff}
compares the raw and intrinsic efficiencies of the flush simulation with the
printed CSV values; Table~\ref{tab:source_eff} lists the numbers.
The flush simulation agrees with the CSV at \SI{46.54}{keV}, \SI{661.64}{keV},
\SI{1408.01}{keV}, and \SI{59.54}{keV} after the \SI{1}{\micro\second} pulse
clustering (\(R=\num{1.058}\), \num{1.100}, \num{1.079}, and \num{1.202}); the
\SI{32.00}{keV} line is \SI{15.90}{\percent} against \SI{18.50}{\percent}
(\(R=\num{0.859}\)), and the \ce{^{60}Co} lines remain high (\(R=\num{1.749}\)
and \num{1.261}).

The \SI{59.54}{keV} value changed most when the analysis moved from an
event sum to pulse counting. Summing all hits in an event gives
\SI{11.41}{\percent} in the flush geometry. The \texttt{hits} tree stores one
row per shower pulse; merging rows whose leading-edge times differ by at most
\SI{1}{\micro\second} gives \SI{28.61}{\percent}. The \SI{1e4}{y} cross-check
gives \SI{28.06}{\percent}; the \num{0.56}-percentage-point difference is the
late-chain contribution. In the \SI{1e60}{y} file, \num{2204} of the
\num{101387} rows in the \SI{59.54}{keV} window have \(t\ge\SI{1e21}{ns}\), so
those pulses come from \ce{^{237}Np} or its descendants rather than from the
primary decay. They are separate in time from the prompt pulse, so the
clustering does not remove them; it prevents them from being summed with it.
The \SI{1e60}{y} run is a full-chain stress test; for a fresh \ce{^{241}Am}
source the \SI{1e4}{y} result is the physical one.

The \ce{^{60}Co} lines remain high after the pulse clustering. This is not a
missing-cascade effect: the flush \ce{^{60}Co} simulation includes the cascade,
so the \SI{1173.23}{keV} and \SI{1332.51}{keV} peaks already include coincidence
summing and the \SI{2505.74}{keV} sum peak is present. At \SI{3}{mm} the
summing-out is strong, but the simulation still gives
\SI{0.892}{\percent} and \SI{0.706}{\percent} against \SI{0.51}{\percent} and
\SI{0.56}{\percent} in the CSV. The flush hypothesis is plausible for some
lines and not for others; no parameter was tuned.

\section{Conclusions}
\begin{enumerate}
\setlength{\itemsep}{0pt}
  \item The geometric solid angle sets a strict physical ceiling $\epsilon\le\epsilon_\Omega(d)$, with upper bounds of \SI{0.0955}{\percent} at \SI{200}{mm}, \SI{0.0428}{\percent} at \SI{300}{mm}, \SI{0.0242}{\percent} at \SI{400}{mm}, and \SI{38.33}{\percent} for the flush-source-to-detector-face geometry ($d=\SI{3}{mm}$).
  \item The \SI{661.64}{keV} simulation/measurement ratio is 0.98--1.03, with pulls between $-0.4$ and $+0.8\sigma$.
  \item The \SI{1332.51}{keV} simulation/measurement ratio is 0.92--1.11, with pulls between $-0.8$ and $+1.8\sigma$. The \SI{1173.23}{keV} ratio is 1.19--1.36, with pulls between $+2.0$ and $+4.4\sigma$. The measured \SI{1173}{keV}/\SI{1332}{keV} ratio is 1.00--1.04, compared with 1.18 in the simulation. The line-dependent excess is not cascade summing at 200--400 mm; the Co-60 source geometry or the detector response model is the more likely cause.
  \item The distance-comparison efficiencies are from Gaussian-plus-linear fits to the 2026-10-27 CLYC2 spectra. The \SI{1173.23}{keV} Gaussian area has a \SI{10}{\percent} background-model systematic from the sideband check; the \SI{661.64}{keV} and \SI{1332.51}{keV} areas agree with the sideband method to about \SI{1}{\percent}. No external efficiency table is used for the distance comparison.
  \item The flush-source simulation agrees with the \SI{2}{cm} calibration file at \SI{46.54}{keV}, \SI{661.64}{keV}, \SI{1408.01}{keV}, and, after the \SI{1}{\micro\second} pulse clustering, \SI{59.54}{keV}; the \SI{121.78}{keV}, \SI{344.28}{keV} and \ce{^{60}Co} lines remain high, so source position alone does not explain the scale difference.
  \item The detector resolution is \(R(E)=(123.9\pm0.4)/\sqrt{E/\mathrm{keV}}\,\%\) (FWHM \(=1.239\sqrt{E/\mathrm{keV}}\,\mathrm{keV}\)) over \SI{661}{keV}--\SI{1332}{keV}. The resolution-smeared \SI{200}{mm} simulation reproduces the \SI{661.64}{keV} peak within about \SI{5}{\percent} and the \SI{1332.51}{keV} peak within about \SI{5}{\percent}; the \SI{1173.23}{keV} full-energy peak area is 19--36\% larger than the measurement.
\end{enumerate}
