\author{J.~Turko}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We describe a \gfour\ (v11.3.2) simulation of a
\SI{250}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{250}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{10}{\milli\meter} polished
plastic scintillator (PVT, UPS-92S/EJ-200 class) read out by side-coupled
silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs), modelled on the muon-trigger detector of
Rao \emph{et al.}\ [J.~Appl.\ Phys.\ \textbf{138}, 114504 (2025)]. The detector
is exposed to a vertical \SI{4}{\giga\electronvolt} muon pencil beam. We document
the geometry and the optical-physics implementation in detail, and state the
actual input data rather than blanket assumptions: the \gfour\ \emph{unified}
surface model and the finish/type combinations that represent the four wrapping
configurations (bare plate in a black holder, TiO$_2$ paint, aluminium foil,
high-gloss paper); the digitized scintillator emission and bulk-absorption
curves; the digitized Kuraray Y-11 WLS absorption and re-emission spectra used by
the config-4 fibre core (with absorption length \SI{1.0}{\milli\meter} at
\SI{430}{\nano\meter}); the digitized ASD-NUV4S-P-40 photocathode PDE that drives
photodetection; and the per-event optical accounting that feeds the analysis.
Config~4 is built from an analytic, multi-clad Y-11 fibre bundle in a white
Lambertian clamp with exponential (\SI{7}{\nano\second}) WLS re-emission.
A \num{48}-file matrix scan (4 SiPM layouts $\times$ 4 reflectors $\times$
3 beam positions, \num{2500} events each, \num{120000} events) and a
whole-plate irradiation (\num{25000} events per configuration and wrapper,
\num{400000} events) are complemented by a \num{63}-file bare-contact scan
(\num{63000} events) and an \num{18}-file aluminium-backing scan
(\num{18000} events), for \num{601000} events in total.
The results reproduce only part of the experimentally observed ordering. The
glossy-paper wrapper is the brightest in every layout, while the diffuse
TiO$_2$ paint is the most position-dependent, but the bare plate is \emph{not}
the weakest collector: modelled with its true \SI{1.2}{\milli\meter} air gap to
the black holder (contact fraction $f=0$ by default), the polished plate is a
near-lossless total-internal-reflection light guide, second in brightness only
to glossy paper in every layout and nearly uniform over the whole plate
($\kappa_{\rm 2D}=\num{1.13}$--\num{1.49}; TiO$_2$ \num{1.90}--\num{8.19},
Al \num{1.18}--\num{1.77}, glossy \num{1.07}--\num{1.25}). A contact-fraction
scan gives $f\approx\num{0.017}$ at the centre and lower values at the
edge/corner for configs~1 and~2, while an air-backed aluminium model reaches
$\approx\SI{92}{\percent}$ of the glossy yield. We report per-event photon yields, photoelectron spectra,
wavelength distributions, position-resolved efficiency maps, and the
contact/aluminium systematics. Model and implementation caveats are documented,
principally the bare-plate ordering discrepancy above.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and motivation}
% =====================================================================

Plastic scintillators read out at their edges by SiPMs are attractive for
large-area, low-cost muon triggers and veto systems for cosmic-ray muon
tomography~\cite{Borozdin2003,Bonechi2020}. The amount of scintillation
light that reaches the photosensor is governed almost entirely by how the
non-instrumented faces of the plate are treated: a polished plate in air traps
light by total internal reflection (TIR) and can be a nearly lossless light
guide, whereas a plate wrapped in an absorbing or diffusive material loses
photons at every bounce. Understanding this trade-off quantitatively is the
central design question, and it is precisely where simulation is most useful,
because the photon transport is a multi-bounce random walk that is difficult to
model analytically~\cite{Korpachev2017}.

This report documents a \gfour\ (v11.3.2)~\cite{Agostinelli2003,Allison2016}
simulation built to address that
question for the specific geometry and surface treatments of Rao \emph{et
al.}~\cite{Rao2025}. The deliverable is a self-contained ``matrix scan'': every
combination of four SiPM layouts and four reflector treatments, at three beam
positions on the plate. The goal is not to fit a single number but to test how
far the simulation reproduces the \emph{ordering} of light-collection efficiency
that the experiment observes, and to expose the physics behind both the parts it
reproduces and the part it does not.

The remainder of this document is organised as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:detector} describes the detector model: the scintillator, the
SiPM read-out, the four read-out layouts (including the wavelength-shifting
fibre clamp of config~4), the reflector treatments, the tabulated optical inputs
that the transport actually consumes (emission, bulk and WLS attenuation,
refractive index, reflector reflectivity, wavelength-shifting and photocathode
PDE), and the optical-physics implementation, all verified line-by-line against
the \gfour\ source.
Section~\ref{sec:matrix} presents the matrix simulation---its beam and patch
setup, the resulting collection efficiencies, photon/photoelectron spectra, and
position-resolved maps---and the two focused campaigns that bracket the
bare-plate holder contact and the aluminium-foil backing.
Section~\ref{sec:whole} presents the complementary whole-slab irradiation, which
samples the full plate uniformly.
Section~\ref{sec:caveats} documents known caveats, and
Sec.~\ref{sec:summary} summarises.

\section{Detector model}
\label{sec:detector}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Scintillator plate}

The active volume is a rectangular PVT scintillator
$250 \times 250 \times \SI{10}{\milli\meter}$, mirroring the UPS-92S plate of
Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}. It is assigned the \gfour\ NIST base material
\texttt{G4\_PLASTIC\_SC\_VINYLTOLUENE} with a custom optical properties table
built from the EJ-200 and UPS-923A data
sheets~\cite{EljenEJ200,Artikov2005,AmcrysPS}. The optical constants---the
refractive index ($n = 1.58$, essentially flat across the visible), light yield,
emission peak, bulk attenuation length, scintillation decay and rise times, and
the Birks constant~\cite{Birks1951,Craun1970}---are collected in the
consolidated parameter table of Sec.~\ref{sec:detparams} and are not repeated
here. Two entries are deliberate modelling choices rather than quoted numbers:
the bulk attenuation length is peaked at \SI{3.8}{\meter} but falls steeply in
the UV to model wavelength-shifter self-absorption, and the emission spectrum
(Fig.~\ref{fig:spectra_opt}) is essentially zero below \SI{380}{\nano\meter} and
above \SI{550}{\nano\meter}. Both scintillation and \v{C}erenkov photons are
produced and tracked (Sec.~\ref{sec:physics}); the optical grid spans
\SIrange{310}{700}{\nano\meter} so that the \v{C}erenkov continuum, which extends
well below the scintillation band, is covered.

\subsection{SiPM readout and coupling}

Each SiPM~\cite{Gundacker2020} is modelled mechanically as a
$4 \times 4 \times \SI{0.6}{\milli\meter}$ box of ``SiPM epoxy'' ($n = 1.55$),
coupled to a side face of the plate through a
\SI{0.1}{\milli\meter} silicone grease pad ($n = 1.46$). The coupling chain is
therefore \emph{scintillator\,$\to$\,grease\,$\to$\,SiPM}, with Fresnel
transmission at each dielectric interface and photodetection at the final
grease/SiPM boundary.

Four layouts are studied (schematic in Fig.~\ref{fig:layouts}):
\begin{description}
  \item[Config 1 (8 SiPMs)] two SiPMs per side face, at $\pm 50\%$ of the half-length.
  \item[Config 2 (4 SiPMs)] one SiPM per side face, at the \emph{same} $\pm 50\%$
        offsets as config~1---i.e.\ config~1 with one device removed per side,
        the removed slot rotated from side to side so the layout remains
        four-fold symmetric.
  \item[Config 3 (2 SiPMs)] both SiPMs on the \emph{same} narrow
        face---the $-x$ face in the simulation frame, which is the report
        plan view's top edge (Sec.~\ref{sec:matrix})---at $\pm 50\%$, matching
        T1/T2 of Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}.
  \item[Config 4 (1 SiPM $+$ WLS clamp)] a single SiPM reading out a
        wavelength-shifting (WLS) fibre clamp along the $-x$ narrow face
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:wls}).
\end{description}
SiPM footprints are clamped so they cannot overhang the slab face.

\subsection{Reflector treatments}
\label{sec:reflectors}

Four treatments are applied as a \emph{logical skin} over the non-instrumented
surfaces of the plate; the grease/SiPM patches override the skin locally with
dedicated \emph{border} surfaces. The mapping from each physical treatment to the
\gfour\ optical-surface parameters is the heart of the model---it determines the
light-collection ordering---so it is given in full. Table~\ref{tab:surfaces}
lists every property that is actually attached to each surface, and the
subsubsections below describe each reflector physically, state how it is
modelled, and assess how faithfully the model represents the real object. (A
fifth finish, PTFE tape, exists in the code for completeness but is not part of
the reference study.) The strong sensitivity of light collection to the wrapping
and reflector choice is well established
experimentally~\cite{Taheri2017}.

\subsubsection{Bare plate in its black holder}
\label{sec:refl-bare}

\emph{Physical object.} The polished UPS-92S plate is clamped into a black
3D-printed ABS enclosure, its faces simply resting near the wall.

\emph{Model.} No wrapping skin is attached. The plate keeps its native polished
scintillator ($n=1.58$) against air ($n=1.0003$) across a real
\SI{1.2}{\milli\meter} gap; light below the critical angle
($\arcsin(1/1.58)=\SI{39.3}{\degree}$) refracts across the gap and is absorbed in
the holder, whose black ABS has an attenuation length of
\SI{1}{\micro\meter}, while light above it is returned by total internal
reflection. Fresnel reflection and TIR are taken from the material
\texttt{RINDEX} tables.

\emph{Fidelity.} This is the least faithful configuration and the source of the
report's central discrepancy. The default model assumes the plate is optically
\emph{decoupled} from the holder everywhere ($f=0$). A real clamped plate
touches the holder at scattered high spots, and each contact forms a
frustrated-TIR sink that removes trapped light; surface scratches do the same.
Those loss channels are absent at $f=0$, so the modelled plate acts as a
near-lossless light guide and is one of the \emph{brightest} collectors, whereas
the reference measurement gives it by far the \emph{lowest} signal. The code
offers an optional mean-field contact fraction,
\texttt{/dcs-monitor/det/muonScint/setBareContactFraction <f>}
($0\le f\le 1$, default $0$): for $f>0$ every hit on a bare face is absorbed
with probability $f$ (a polished \texttt{dielectric\_dielectric} skin with
\texttt{REFLECTIVITY} $=1-f$ and \texttt{TRANSMITTANCE} $=0$), representing
frustrated TIR over an effective contact area. The loss is ignored for the
config-4 bare layout, whose clamp occupies that face. A dedicated $f$ scan has
been performed in the production campaign and is summarised in
Sec.~\ref{sec:contact}; the clean air-gap model ($f=0$) remains the default
because it is the defensible reading of the stated construction (a plate set in
a holder).

\subsubsection{Titanium-dioxide paint (EJ-510)}
\label{sec:refl-tio2}

\emph{Physical object.} A white, diffuse-reflectance paint applied directly to
the plate (three coats, $\approx\SI{0.11}{\milli\meter}$), in optical contact
with the scintillator.

\emph{Model.} \texttt{groundfrontpainted} with a purely Lambertian reflection: a
random draw against \texttt{REFLECTIVITY} returns the photon with a cosine-law
distribution and \emph{no} specular or TIR component
($f_{ss}=f_{sl}=f_{bs}=0$). The reflectance rises from \num{0.955} in the UV to
a plateau of \num{0.983} near \SI{470}{\nano\meter}.

\emph{Fidelity.} The Lambertian/no-TIR treatment is the physically correct
description of light emerging from a thick, strongly scattering paint, and is
this wrapper's strongest feature. The \emph{absolute} reflectance curve is its
weakest: no public spectral-reflectance data exist for EJ-510, so $R(\lambda)$
is a fitted representative white-paint curve ($\approx 0.96$--$0.98$), not a
measurement of the actual coating, and no angular dependence of the diffuse
lobe is modelled. The \texttt{sigma\_alpha} value is set for bookkeeping but is
inert at this finish (\texttt{groundfrontpainted} reflects diffusely regardless
of the facet normal).

\subsubsection{Aluminium foil}
\label{sec:refl-al}

\emph{Physical object.} Commercial shiny aluminium foil wrapped around the
plate; per Ref.~\cite{Rao2025} optical grease is applied in places so that the
foil contacts the scintillator rather than leaving an air gap.

\emph{Model.} \texttt{polishedbackpainted} with a selectable backing refractive
index, chosen pre-init with
\texttt{/dcs-monitor/det/muonScint/setAlBacking air|grease}. The default,
\texttt{grease} ($n=1.46$), represents a foil applied over the coupling grease,
as in Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}; the scintillator/grease interface is a normal
Fresnel/TIR boundary, but since the index step is small the critical angle is
wide ($\arcsin(1.46/1.58)=\SI{67.5}{\degree}$): most light refracts into the
grease film and is returned by the foil as a pure \emph{specular} reflection
($f_{ss}=1$). The alternative, \texttt{air} ($n=1.0003$), leaves the full TIR
cone intact and lets only the sub-critical fraction cross to the foil, modelling
a foil separated from the plate by a trapped air layer. In both cases
\texttt{REFLECTIVITY} is the normal-incidence reflectance of evaporated
aluminium computed from the McPeak \emph{et al.}~$n,k$
data~\cite{McPeak2015} (\num{0.904} at \SI{650}{\nano\meter} rising to
\num{0.925} at \SI{360}{\nano\meter}).

\emph{Fidelity.} Two idealisations. (i) The tabulated reflectance is that of a
smooth evaporated film and is therefore an \emph{upper bound}: household foil is
rougher and carries a native oxide, both of which lower $R$ and add a small
diffuse component we do not model. (ii) The foil is assumed to cover the plate
uniformly, whereas real wrapping leaves wrinkles and trapped air. Which backing
choice is appropriate is itself an open question: the production whole-slab runs
use the \texttt{grease} default, whose tight critical angle leaves the foil
third, below the bare plate, whereas the \texttt{air} backing recovers the
measured near-equality of aluminium and glossy paper, reaching
$\approx\SI{92}{\percent}$ of the glossy yield.

\subsubsection{High-gloss photo paper}
\label{sec:refl-glossy}

\emph{Physical object.} A commercial super-high-gloss photographic paper
(Print4Life, \SI{300}{\gram\per\square\meter}) wrapped around the plate with an
air gap (no grease).

\emph{Model.} \texttt{polishedbackpainted} with the backing index set to air
($n=1.0003$), so the plate retains its full TIR cone and only the sub-critical
fraction crosses the gap to the paper, which returns it as a specular reflection
($f_{ss}=1$) with a fitted $R(\lambda)\approx 0.900$--$0.955$---slightly lossier
than the aluminium.

\emph{Fidelity.} The reference reports glossy paper and aluminium foil as the two
best wrappers (large, nearly equal signals), which motivates modelling glossy
paper as a \emph{slightly lossy mirror} rather than a diffuser. This is a model
choice rather than a measurement: real photo paper is not a true mirror and has
a diffuse component, and its $R(\lambda)$ is again fitted (no measured data),
so this is the reflector whose optical model is least constrained by independent
data.

\subsection{Config 4: wavelength-shifting fibre clamp}
\label{sec:wls}

The fourth layout follows the single-SiPM, WLS-readout variant of
Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}. Four \SI{1.0}{\milli\meter}-diameter Kuraray Y-11(200)
multi-clad wavelength-shifting fibres~\cite{KurarayPSF,Alekseev2022} run the full
\SI{250}{\milli\meter} length of the $-x$ narrow face in a single row. Over most
of the length they are parallel at a \SI{2.5}{\milli\meter} centre-to-centre
pitch---so a solid web of clamp material separates the milled
\SI{1.1}{\milli\meter} channels---and converge over a \SI{41}{\milli\meter}
length (\SI{39}{\milli\meter} ramp plus a \SI{2}{\milli\meter} stub) to a
touching \SI{1.0}{\milli\meter} bundle that presents
all four ends to a single \SI{4}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{4}{\milli\meter} SiPM at
the $-y$ end; their far $+y$ ends are closed by individual aluminium mirror caps.
Each fibre is modelled as a multi-clad guide: a Y-11 core ($n = 1.59$) inside a
PMMA inner cladding ($n = 1.49$) inside a low-index fluoropolymer outer sheath
($n = 1.42$)---the low-index staircase that sustains core--cladding total
internal reflection. In the production geometry the fibre solid is an analytic,
mitered set of \texttt{G4CutTubs} legs (one per straight segment of the bent
centreline), placed so adjoining legs meet at exact miter planes; this
facet-free construction is what the runs use. (An earlier tessellated swept
cylinder, which occasionally produced stuck optical tracks, is retained as an
optional visualisation-only variant.) A thin \SI{0.10}{\milli\meter} grease film
couples the plate face to the clamp, and each fibre protrudes
\SI{0.05}{\milli\meter} through its channel mouth into that film, keeping the
grease continuous against the scintillator. The deliberate
\SI{0.05}{\milli\meter} air annulus between the \SI{1.0}{\milli\meter} fibre and
its \SI{1.1}{\milli\meter} channel is essential: the fibre must not touch the
clamp ($n = 1.55$), and the outer sheath ($n = 1.42$) needs the low-index
clearance to keep guiding the light. The clamp body is 3D-printed white plastic,
modelled by default as a Lambertian reflector with \SI{85}{\percent} reflectivity
applied to \emph{every} clamp face (channel walls and grease-facing front
included), so light leaking out of the fibre is returned rather than lost. The
far-end mirror is a full aluminium disc whose logical volume carries a reflecting
skin as well as the fibre--cap border surfaces, so that light travelling in the
\emph{core}---not only the cladding---is also turned around at the far end. WLS
re-emission is drawn from the digitized Y-11 emission spectrum with an
exponential delay of \SI{7}{\nano\second} (Sec.~\ref{sec:wlphysics}), and the
\texttt{G4OpWLS} process is enabled automatically when this geometry is built. In
the report's top-down frame the clamp runs along the \emph{top} edge with the
single read-out SiPM at the \emph{top-left} corner, as in Fig.~3(b) of
Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}.

\subsection{Optical physics implementation}
\label{sec:physics}

Optical photons are transported by the standard \gfour\ optical physics
module~\cite{Geant4PRM}, with scintillation, \v{C}erenkov, bulk
absorption, Rayleigh scattering and boundary processes all active. Multiple
scattering (Mie) is disabled; wavelength shifting (the \texttt{G4OpWLS} process)
is enabled only for the config-4 fibre layout (Sec.~\ref{sec:wls}) and inert for
all others. This subsection describes
the boundary physics and the photodetection model, and states explicitly the
\gfour\ behaviour that each modelling choice relies on, verified against the
\gfour\ source in \texttt{.refs/source/processes/optical}.

\subsection{The boundary process and the unified surface model}

Photon crossing of a dielectric interface is handled by
\texttt{G4OpBoundaryProcess}, which selects an \texttt{G4OpticalSurface} from
the unified surface model of Levin and Moisan~\cite{Levin1996} (whose key
parameters for Geant4/GATE photon transport were later
characterised~\cite{Nilsson2015}), using
(\emph{i}) a \texttt{G4LogicalBorderSurface} between two physical volumes or
(\emph{ii}) a \texttt{G4LogicalSkinSurface} on a logical volume. Reading the
source (lines 319--347), the lookup is: a border surface between the pre- and
post-step volumes is consulted first; if none exists, the skin surface of the
daughter (or, failing that, the mother) logical volume is used. This establishes
two facts on which the geometry relies:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a \texttt{G4LogicalBorderSurface} takes precedence over a
        \texttt{G4LogicalSkinSurface} at the same boundary---hence the grease
        and SiPM patches punch ``holes'' in the wrapping skin;
  \item if no surface object exists at a boundary, the process falls back to
        the default lossless TIR behaviour (via \texttt{DielectricDielectric()}).
\end{enumerate}

The boundary process requires a valid \texttt{RINDEX}: on medium~1 always, and
on medium~2 for a \texttt{polished}/\texttt{ground}
\texttt{dielectric\_dielectric} boundary (lines 289--306, 436--453);
\texttt{*backpainted} finishes instead read the backing index from the
\emph{surface} MPT (lines 356--374). If the property is absent the process sets
status \texttt{NoRINDEX} and kills the photon \emph{silently}---the message is
printed only above verbose level~1---which is why every material (and every
backpainted surface) in the model carries an explicit \texttt{RINDEX} table.
This silent kill is also why the far-end fibre mirror needed an explicit skin
(Sec.~\ref{sec:wls}): the metal cap has no \texttt{RINDEX}, so the un-covered
core-to-cap crossing was being lost.

For the \emph{unified} model the probability weights for the reflection
sub-models are read from the surface MPT---\texttt{SPECULARSPIKECONSTANT}
($f_{ss}$), \texttt{SPECULARLOBECONSTANT} ($f_{sl}$) and
\texttt{BACKSCATTERCONSTANT} ($f_{bs}$)---together with \texttt{REFLECTIVITY}
(line 381) and, where applicable, \texttt{TRANSMITTANCE} (line 394) and
\texttt{EFFICIENCY} (line 390). The surviving probability
$1 - f_{ss} - f_{sl} - f_{bs}$ is shared between diffuse (Lambertian) and bare
reflection.

\subsection{Reflector finishes}

The four treatments use three distinct code paths in
\texttt{G4OpBoundaryProcess}, which we verified:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{groundfrontpainted}} (TiO$_2$). The finish is handled by
        the ``painted-front'' branch (lines 462--488): a uniform random draw
        against \texttt{REFLECTIVITY} decides absorbed / transmitted / reflected,
        and reflection is set to a pure Lambertian distribution
        (\texttt{LambertianReflection}, line 481). There is \emph{no} TIR at this
        finish---photons are either diffusely reflected or absorbed. This is the
        physically correct description of sub-surface scattering in a thick
        TiO$_2$ paint.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{polishedbackpainted}} (Al, glossy paper). This finish is
        routed to \texttt{DielectricDielectric()} (line 456--459). The
        scintillator/backing interface still exhibits Fresnel reflection and TIR;
        sub-critical photons cross it and are returned by the backing ``paint''
        with reflectivity $R(\lambda)$ and a reflection distribution set by
        $f_{ss}, f_{sl}, f_{bs}$. We choose $f_{ss} = 1$ (pure specular spike),
        since both wrappers are smooth mirrors, and set the backing refractive
        index to distinguish the two physical situations. For the aluminium foil
        the backing index is the coupling grease ($n = 1.46$), following
        Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}, which applies grease so the foil optically contacts
        the plate; the critical angle is then only
        $\arcsin(1.46/1.58) = \SI{67.5}{\degree}$, so most light refracts out of
        the plate and is returned specularly by the foil, whose reflectivity is
        the tabulated normal-incidence reflectance of evaporated aluminium. For
        glossy paper the backing index is that of air ($n = 1.0003$), so the
        plate keeps its full TIR cone and only the sub-critical fraction reaches
        the paper, which acts as a slightly lossier mirror.
  \item \textbf{Bare plate}. This is deliberately \emph{not} given a wrapping
        skin. The plate keeps its plain polished boundary across a real
        \SI{1.2}{\milli\meter} air gap to the holder shell. The sub-critical
        fraction refracts across that gap and is then killed by the holder: the
        black shell carries an absorbing skin ($R = T = 0$), and its bulk
        (\SI{1}{\micro\meter} absorption length) would absorb the light anyway.
        Super-critical light is retained by TIR. There is \emph{no} absorbing
        skin on the plate itself; the same air-gap loss is applied at the
        read-out pads, which are separated from the plate by a thin air border
        in this configuration.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Wavelength shifting}
\label{sec:wlphysics}

In the config-4 layout, photons that reach the fibre cores are absorbed and
re-emitted isotropically at longer wavelength by the \texttt{G4OpWLS} process,
which is configured through the fibre material's \texttt{WLSABSLENGTH},
\texttt{WLSCOMPONENT} and \texttt{WLSTIMECONSTANT} properties. Both spectra are
the digitized Kuraray Y-11(200) curves: the absorption band peaks at
\SI{430}{\nano\meter} (onset $\approx\SI{486}{\nano\meter}$) and the re-emission
peak is \SI{476}{\nano\meter} (Fig.~\ref{fig:spectra_opt}). The absolute
absorption strength is set by the \SI{430}{\nano\meter} absorption length,
\SI{1.0}{\milli\meter} in production (with a systematic band of
\SIrange{0.3}{3}{\milli\meter} available through the
\texttt{setWLSAbsLength} command). The re-emission is sampled with an
exponential time profile, $\tau = \SI{7}{\nano\second}$, rather than a fixed
delay. \texttt{G4OpWLS} samples the secondary from \texttt{WLSCOMPONENT} subject
to $E_\gamma' \le E_\gamma$, so the emission table is cut at the absorption
onset; a small overlap of the emission tail with the absorption band gives mild
self-absorption. The process is switched on by the physics list when the WLS
geometry is detected (the \gfour\ optical-physics constructor reads the process
activation at build time, so the flag is set before the base construction).
Because most of the shifted emission lies red-ward of the absorption band, the
re-emitted light is transported along the fibre by TIR to the read-out
SiPM---which is why the detected config-4 spectrum is green-shifted (mean
$\sim\!\SI{510}{\nano\meter}$) relative to the direct-coupled configurations.

\subsection{Photodetection: the SiPM photocathode}

Photodetection is delegated entirely to \gfour, rather than being drawn
manually in the sensitive detector. The grease/SiPM boundary is a
\texttt{dielectric\_metal} surface whose MPT carries the SiPM
\texttt{EFFICIENCY} as a function of photon energy (equivalently, of the
wavelength $\lambda$), i.e.\ the photon detection
efficiency (PDE) of an ASD-NUV4S-P-40 device~\cite{AdvanSiD_NUV}, peaking at
$0.431$ near \SI{410}{\nano\meter} (the digitized 25-point curve of
Fig.~\ref{fig:spectra_opt}; values fall to $\approx 0.30$ at
\SI{490}{\nano\meter}), with \texttt{REFLECTIVITY} $=0$.

The mechanism is worth stating precisely because it is the reason the sensitive
detector needs no random draw of its own. In \texttt{DielectricMetal()}
(lines 724--817), when the Fresnel--PDE roll places a photon in the ``absorbed''
channel, \texttt{DoAbsorption()} is called. Inside it (header, lines 305--320):
\begin{equation}
  f_{\mathrm{status}} =
  \begin{cases}
    \mathrm{Absorption}, & \xi \ge \operatorname{EFFICIENCY}(\lambda),\\[2pt]
    \mathrm{Detection},  & \xi <  \operatorname{EFFICIENCY}(\lambda),
  \end{cases}
  \qquad \xi \sim U(0,1),
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{EFFICIENCY}(\lambda)$ is the tabulated PDE. The absorbed
photon is assigned a non-zero
\texttt{LocalEnergyDeposit} \emph{only} when the PDE draw succeeds. Back in
\texttt{PostStepDoIt}, if the status is \texttt{Detection} \emph{and} the
invoke-SD flag is set, the sensitive detector is invoked (lines 559--560):
\begin{equation}
  f_{\mathrm{status}} = \mathrm{Detection}\ \wedge\ f_{\mathrm{InvokeSD}}
  \ \Longrightarrow\ \operatorname{InvokeSD}(\text{step}).
\end{equation}
The flag is enabled in the physics list via
\texttt{G4OpticalParameters::SetBoundaryInvokeSD(true)}. Consequently the
sensitive-detector \texttt{ProcessHits} fires \emph{only} for genuine
photoelectrons (it tests that the step energy deposit is positive), counts one
photoelectron per call, and kills the photon (the SiPM is opaque). This is a clean
separation of concerns: the physics of the photocathode lives in the optical
surface, and the sensitive detector only books hits.

\subsection{Per-event optical accounting}

Two per-event counts recur throughout this report. Write $\pe^{(e,s)}$ for the
number of photoelectrons detected in SiPM $s$ during event $e$, and
$\ngam^{(e)}$ for the number of optical photons generated in the slab during
event $e$ (scintillation plus \v{C}erenkov), irrespective of their later fate.

A thread-local diagnostic record (\texttt{OpticalDiag}) accumulates, per event:
scintillation photons, \v{C}erenkov photons, terminated photons, detected
photoelectrons, and photons that escaped to the world. The primary muon's
first-entry position in the slab is captured on the first step inside the
scintillator logical volume and stored in slab-local coordinates. At end of
event the sensitive detector writes one row per (event, SiPM), \emph{including
zero-detection rows}, so the sample is unbiased in the muon interaction
position. Each row carries the entry position, the event's produced-photon
count (the efficiency denominator), the detected count in that SiPM, the first
arrival time, the mean and RMS detected wavelength, and the track weight. Three
variable-length branches additionally list the individual photoelectrons: their
global arrival times (\texttt{tDetected}), wavelengths
(\texttt{wlDetected\_nm}) and creating processes (\texttt{creatorDetected}, with
$1 =$ \texttt{OpWLS}, $2 =$ scintillation, $3 =$ \v{C}erenkov). The per-row
count, timing and spectral data therefore support per-SiPM, combined and
spatially-binned collection-efficiency analyses together with offline
pulse-shape and process-mix studies.

scintillator---with the scintillation yield rescaled, so that the individual
optical tracks can be followed: photons are generated along the muon path,
propagate by total internal reflection and specular reflection off the wrapper,
and terminate on the SiPM patches at the four edges.

\subsection{Beam, irradiation patches, and efficiency definition}

The primary source is a vertical muon pencil beam: a \SI{50}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{50}{\milli\meter}
square beam of \SI{4}{\giga\electronvolt} $\mu^-$ normally incident on the plate,
with the beam centre placed at three positions, each irradiating a
\SI{50}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{50}{\milli\meter} patch lying \emph{entirely
on} the polished plate. With the plate spanning $[-125,125]\,\si{\milli\meter}$
in both $x$ and $y$, the patch centres are
\[
  \text{centre} = (0,0),\qquad
  \text{edge} = (0,-100),\qquad
  \text{corner} = (100,-100)\ \si{\milli\meter},
\]
so the illuminated regions are $[-25,25]^2$, $[-25,25]\times[-125,-75]$ and
$[75,125]\times[-125,-75]$ respectively (report frame: $+y$ is the top,
$-x$ the left). This places the edge and corner regions on the bottom
($-y$) edge, opposite the single instrumented face of config~3 (top, $+y$),
as in Fig.~3 of Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}. It also matches the \SI{50}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{50}{\milli\meter}
active area of the muon coincidence detector (MCD) that defines the regions of
interest in Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}, and keeps the irradiated area---and hence the
denominator of Eq.~\ref{eq:eff}---equal at all three positions. Each
(config, reflector, position) runs \num{2500} events after a single
\texttt{/run/initialize}. The matrix therefore comprises
\[
  4\ \text{configs} \times 4\ \text{reflectors} \times 3\ \text{positions}
  = 48\ \text{files}, \qquad 48 \times \num{2500} = \num{120000}\ \text{events}.
\]
Two focused campaigns complete the production dataset. A bare-contact scan
runs configs~1--3 at seven contact fractions $f=0$, $0.005$, \dots, $0.030$ at
the three beam positions, $3 \times 7 \times 3 = \num{63}$ files with
\num{1000} events each (\num{63000} events); an aluminium-backing scan runs
configs~1--3 with \texttt{grease} and \texttt{air} backing at the three
positions, $3 \times 2 \times 3 = \num{18}$ files with \num{1000} events each
(\num{18000} events). Together with the whole-plate run of
Sec.~\ref{sec:whole} ($16 \times \num{25000} = \num{400000}$ events), the
campaign totals \num{601000} events, of which \num{201000} are matrix-style
($\text{matrix} + \text{contact} + \text{Al}$).

Production runs were executed on the HPC cluster.
The optional per-slab optical track-length flux map is disabled for the matrix
(it is retained as a separate diagnostic); the collection efficiency is
reconstructed entirely offline from the \texttt{sipmHits} tree at any desired
binning, so the map is not needed.

The collection efficiency is defined, for a set $\mathcal{S}$ of SiPMs and an
event $e$, as
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{e}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}} \pe^{(e,s)}}
                     {\displaystyle\sum_{e}\ngam^{(e)}},
  \label{eq:eff}
\end{equation}
where the outer sum runs over all simulated events and the inner sum over the
SiPM set $\mathcal{S}$. The all-SiPM sum over the three beam
positions is the per-position figure of merit. A \emph{plate-averaged}
efficiency is the appropriate global figure of merit, but the three patches
sample only part of the plate; it is therefore obtained from the uniform
whole-plate irradiation of Sec.~\ref{sec:whole} and reported there.

For presentation, all position-resolved results (the maps of
Fig.~\ref{fig:irr} and the whole-slab maps
of Sec.~\ref{sec:whole}) are shown in the report frame with the top edge at $+y$,
the bottom at $-y$, the left at $-x$ and the right at $+x$. The internal
simulation frame is this view rotated by \SI{90}{\degree} clockwise; the primary
muon entry points and the SiPM markers are mapped into the report frame by
$(x,y)\to(y,-x)$ before plotting. This rotation is a rigid change of view and
leaves every scalar result (yields, spectra, efficiencies) unchanged; it only
fixes the orientation of config~3 (top edge) and of the edge/corner irradiation
sites (bottom edge).

\subsection{Photon yield and photoelectron spectra}

Figure~\ref{fig:yield} shows the per-event optical-photon yield (the
denominator of Eq.~\ref{eq:eff}) for the four reflectors. The mean yield is
essentially independent of the reflector---about \num{19600} photons per event---which is the correct physical behaviour: the reflector changes how
photons are transported, not how many are produced. The small spread reflects
event-to-event variation in the muon path length.

Figure~\ref{fig:detected} shows the corresponding photoelectron spectra. Here
the reflector has a clear effect: the config-1 centre mean runs from
\num{124.6}\,\pe/event for TiO$_2$ and \num{200.0}\,\pe/event for aluminium up to
\num{303.7}\,\pe/event for the air-gap bare plate and \num{520.6}\,\pe/event for
glossy paper. In particular the bare curve is \emph{not} a near-zero peak but a
broad distribution comparable to the reflective wrappers---the direct signature
of its near-lossless TIR transport.

\subsection{Wavelength of detected photons}

Figure~\ref{fig:wavelength} shows the wavelength distribution of detected
photoelectrons, weighted by their count, for the four reflectors. The
distribution peaks near \SI{456}{\nano\meter} for the reflective wrappers and
the bare plate (measured config-1 centre means of \SI{452.3}{\nano\meter} for
aluminium and \SI{457.0}{\nano\meter} for the bare plate), i.e.\ slightly
red-shifted with respect to the \SI{425}{\nano\meter} emission peak. This is the
expected consequence of self-absorption: the shorter-wavelength part of the
emission overlaps the absorption band and is attenuated over the multi-bounce
path, so the surviving and detected spectrum is red-shifted. With the air-gap
model the bare plate now has as many detected photons as the reflective
wrappers, so its spectrum is equally well defined.

\subsection{Spatial and directional dependence}
\label{sec:spatial}

The matrix scan separates the three \SI{50}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{50}{\milli\meter}
patches (centre, edge, corner) precisely so that the response can be resolved by
position. The tempting single-coordinate summary is the distance $r$ of the patch
from the plate centre, but $r$ is blind to azimuth, and the shortcoming is
decisive here: configs~3 and~4 instrument a \emph{single} face, so the collection
depends on the \emph{direction} from which the plate is irradiated, not merely on
the distance. All three patches also lie on the half of the plate opposite the
instrumented face of the single-sided layouts, so their distances are not even
monotonic in the proximity to a sensor. We therefore keep the three patches
separate and compare them directly.

Figure~\ref{fig:poslayouts} groups the three patches by layout (one panel per
config, four reflector curves) and Fig.~\ref{fig:posrefl} groups them by
reflector (one panel per wrapper, four layout curves). Because every patch lies
fully on the plate and all three have equal area, the produced-photon
denominators are equal and the three points within each curve are directly
comparable. The curves are plotted on a logarithmic $y$-axis, which resolves the
weaker TiO$_2$ wrapper alongside the others.

The conspicuous feature is that the bare plate and the reflective wrappers are
nearly \emph{flat} across the three patches: across all four layouts the
maximum/minimum patch ratio spans \SIrange{1.8}{5.6}{\percent} for the air-gap
bare plate, \SIrange{4.5}{8.7}{\percent} for aluminium and
\SIrange{0.8}{2.9}{\percent} for glossy paper. Only the diffuse TiO$_2$ paint
shows a clear position dependence, varying by \SIrange{75}{84}{\percent} (a
factor \num{1.75}--\num{1.84}). This is the
expected behaviour of a closed cavity: after the first few bounces the light no
longer remembers where the muon crossed the plate, so the sensor coverage, and
not the irradiation point, sets the collection. The specular foil and glossy
paper homogenise the response directly; the air-gap bare plate homogenises it
more slowly, but because its bulk attenuation is weak the TIR random walk mixes
the light just as thoroughly by the time it reaches the edges.

These three patches lie near the \emph{instrumented} half of the plate, so the
comparison above captures the position dependence within the well-lit region.
The response over the \emph{whole} plate is resolved in
Sec.~\ref{sec:wholeunif}; there the bare and specular wrappers are confirmed to
be almost uniform, while TiO$_2$ retains the strongest (and now
centre-depressed) position dependence.

\subsection{Bare-plate holder contact}
\label{sec:contact}

The default bare model assumes a clean \SI{1.2}{\milli\meter} air gap between
the polished plate and the black holder. A real clamped plate touches the holder
at scattered high spots, and each contact frustrates total internal reflection
and removes trapped light. To bracket this unmeasured loss channel we repeat the
three-patch scan for a mean-field contact fraction $f$: every hit on a bare face
is absorbed with probability $f$ and otherwise follows the normal Fresnel/TIR
path, implemented as a polished \texttt{dielectric\_dielectric} skin with
\texttt{REFLECTIVITY} $=1-f$ and \texttt{TRANSMITTANCE} $=0$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:refl-bare}). The scan covers configs~1--3, the three beam
positions and seven fractions $f=0$, $0.005$, \dots, $0.030$, with \num{1000}
events per case. It is an effective area fraction, not a measurement of the
holder gap.

Figure~\ref{fig:contact} shows the absolute yield versus $f$, with the matrix
TiO$_2$ reference drawn as dashed lines, and the normalised centre yield. At
$f=0$ the config-1 bare plate collects \num{304.95}, \num{295.37} and
\num{288.55}~pe/event at the centre, edge and corner; the corresponding config-2
values are \num{160.21}, \num{153.68} and \num{152.25}, and the config-3 values
\num{81.27}, \num{79.20} and \num{80.23}. A 1\% contact fraction already removes
about half the light: at the config-1 centre the yield falls to \num{155.01}
pe/event, and at $f=0.030$ to \num{93.61}. The loss is therefore strongly
non-linear in the first few percent of contact area, as expected when a small
fraction of the surface provides a fast escape path for the trapped mode
volume.

\subsection{Aluminium backing: grease versus air}
\label{sec:albacking}

The aluminium foil is the one wrapper whose optical contact is explicitly
ambiguous in the reference construction: grease is applied in places, but
wrinkles and trapped air leave other regions decoupled. The production model
uses the \texttt{grease} backing ($n=1.46$, critical angle
$\SI{67.5}{\degree}$), which lets most trapped light refract into the grease and
return specularly from the foil. The alternative \texttt{air} backing
($n=1.0003$, critical angle $\SI{39.3}{\degree}$ at the scintillator) leaves the
full TIR cone intact, so only the sub-critical fraction reaches the foil. Both
use the same tabulated aluminium reflectance ($R=0.904$--$0.925$). We scanned
both backings for configs~1--3 at the three positions, with \num{1000} events
per case, using \texttt{/dcs-monitor/det/muonScint/setAlBacking air|grease}.

Figure~\ref{fig:albacking} and Table~\ref{tab:albacking} show the result. Air
backing raises the aluminium yield by a factor \num{2.38}--\num{2.59} depending
on layout and position. At the config-1 centre the foil goes from \num{201.92}
to \num{480.05}~pe/event, at the config-2 centre from \num{102.51} to
\num{254.57}, and at the config-3 centre from \num{51.98} to \num{127.90}. The
air-backed foil reaches \SI{92.2}{\percent}, \SI{93.4}{\percent} and
\SI{91.6}{\percent} of the glossy yield in those three cases, i.e.\ it recovers
the measured near-equality of aluminium and glossy paper. The grease-backed foil
instead sits below the bare plate and only slightly above TiO$_2$, the ordering
reported in the plate averages of Table~\ref{tab:eff}. The enhancement is
slightly larger at the edge and corner than at the centre for config~3, but the
position dependence within each backing remains weak.

\subsection{Plate-averaged collection efficiency}

The matrix scan pools only three \SI{50}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{50}{\milli\meter}
patches, all on the $+x$ half of the plate, so the efficiency it yields depends
on the choice of patches and is \emph{not} a plate average. The whole-slab run
removes that bias: the GPS plane is expanded to
$250 \times \SI{250}{\milli\meter}$ (\texttt{halfx} $=$ \texttt{halfy} $=$
\SI{125}{\milli\meter}) and \num{25000} events are generated per
(config, reflector), so the primary muon's first-entry position is sampled
\emph{uniformly} over the entire plate and the pooled ratio
$\sum\pe/\sum\ngam$ is the \emph{plate-averaged} collection efficiency---the
appropriate global figure of merit. Figure~\ref{fig:bars} shows it for every
(config, reflector) pair, and Table~\ref{tab:eff} lists the numbers.

The measured ordering of Ref.~\cite{Rao2025} is only partly reproduced. Glossy
paper is the brightest wrapper in every layout, as the experiment finds, but the
bare plate is \emph{not} the weakest collector: with its true air gap it is the
\emph{second}-brightest configuration in every layout. The resulting orderings
are
\[
  \text{TiO}_2 < \text{Al} < \text{bare} < \text{glossy}
  \quad(\text{configs 1--3}),
  \qquad
  \text{Al} < \text{TiO}_2 < \text{bare} < \text{glossy}
  \quad(\text{config 4}),
\]
so within a given layout the spread from the weakest to the brightest wrapper is
only a factor of about three, and the bare plate sits near the top rather than
at the bottom. The reason is that a polished plate in air is a nearly lossless
TIR light guide (Sec.~\ref{sec:physics}): without a rough or
optically-contacting surface the trapped light is efficiently transported to the
edge sensors, so the bare collection is comparable to---and, for the
grease-coupled foil whose reflectivity is only $\approx\num{90}\%$, higher
than---the aluminium wrapper.
The efficiency scales roughly with the photosensitive area: the eight-SiPM
layout (config~1) is highest, four SiPMs on four faces (config~2) roughly halve
it, and concentrating the two sensors of config~3 on a single face costs a
further factor. The single-SiPM WLS-fibre layout (config~4) collects
substantially less than the direct-coupled layouts: only the fraction of light
that reaches the fibre face is wavelength-shifted and guided to the sensor, so
its efficiency sits roughly a factor of 1.5 to 2.5 below config~3.
In this layout the aluminium foil is no longer more efficient than the diffuse
paint: the TiO$_2$ wrapper overtakes it, so the foil becomes the weakest of the
four, though all four wrappers remain within a factor of about 2.3 of one
another.

\subsection{Full-plate maps}

The same whole-slab events that give the plate average above also resolve the
response by entry position. Reconstructing the efficiency from the
\texttt{sipmHits} tree in $5 \times \SI{5}{\milli\meter}$ cells gives the field
\[
  \varepsilon(x,y) = \frac{\sum_{e\,:\,(x_e,y_e)\in\text{cell}}\ \sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}} \pe^{(e,s)}}
                          {\sum_{e\,:\,(x_e,y_e)\in\text{cell}}\ \ngam^{(e)}},
\]
where the sums run over the whole-slab events whose primary-muon first-entry
position $(x_e,y_e)$ falls inside the cell, i.e.\ identically to
Eq.~\ref{eq:eff} but binned in the entry position. The
whole-slab maps therefore fill in the gaps left by the three-patch scan.

Figure~\ref{fig:wholeslab} shows the complete set of full-plate maps for
config~1, one panel per reflector. For the bare plate the plate-averaged
efficiency is $\varepsilon = 1.556\times10^{-2}$, consistent with the three-patch
bare values of Table~\ref{tab:positions}, and the map is nearly \emph{flat}: the
mean within $r < \SI{60}{\milli\meter}$ ($1.534\times10^{-2}$) differs from that
in the outer region ($1.561\times10^{-2}$) by under \SI{2}{\percent}. This is the
direct consequence of the TIR-guide picture: because bulk attenuation is weak,
photons born at the centre random-walk to a sensor-bearing edge with little loss,
so the entry position barely matters.

The other panels show the same weak position dependence, with TiO$_2$ as the one
exception. Aluminium is essentially flat ($1.026\times10^{-2}$ for
$r < \SI{60}{\milli\meter}$ versus $1.056\times10^{-2}$ beyond) and glossy paper
flatter still ($2.635$ versus $2.652\times10^{-2}$). TiO$_2$ is the only wrapper
with a clear gradient, and it runs the other way: the centre
($6.734\times10^{-3}$) is \emph{dimmer} than the edge and corner
($1.016\times10^{-2}$), a factor of $\sim\!1.51$. Diffuse recycling couples light
into the sensors most efficiently when the source sits near a painted edge, and
least when it is in the middle of the plate, whereas the specular and TIR
transports erase the entry position altogether.

\subsection{Whole-slab uniformity}
\label{sec:wholeunif}

The full-plate maps also expose an inhomogeneity that the three discrete
patches of Sec.~\ref{sec:matrix} can only sample.  Because the whole-slab beam
irradiates every point of the plate, this inhomogeneity can be quantified
\emph{continuously} and with far better statistics.

A purely radial contrast is deliberately \emph{not} used.  The read-out layouts
have no continuous rotational symmetry---configs~1 and~2 are four-fold
symmetric, configs~3 and~4 single-sided---and the plate is square, so the
collection efficiency is not a function of radius alone.  Beyond the inscribed
circle ($r > \SI{125}{\milli\meter}$) only the four corner wedges of the plate
are populated, so a fixed-width radial bin would mix a shrinking range of
directions and its radial profile $\varepsilon(r)$ would reflect the geometry of
the square rather than the detector.  The uniformity is therefore quantified directly in
the plate plane $(x,y)$.

\emph{Two complementary uniformity measures.}  The whole-slab scan gives the
collection efficiency as a \emph{field} $\varepsilon(x,y)$, the position-binned
image of Eq.~\ref{eq:eff}.  We characterise this field in two ways.
(i)~The \emph{normalised map} $\varepsilon/\langle\varepsilon\rangle$, with
$\langle\varepsilon\rangle$ the plate-averaged efficiency, is shown for each
layout in Figs.~\ref{fig:wholeunifmap}--\ref{fig:wholeunifmap4} in
\SI{5}{\milli\meter} cells.  It is a dimensionless field of unit mean that shows
\emph{where} the response is high or low and the spatial pattern of the
asymmetry (for instance a top--bottom gradient), but \emph{not} the absolute
efficiency.
(ii)~The \emph{two-dimensional contrast}
\[
  \kappa_{\rm 2D} \;=\; \frac{\max_{\rm cell}\varepsilon}{\min_{\rm cell}\varepsilon}
\]
collapses the field to a single number, the ratio of the brightest to the
darkest region; it answers ``how large is the spread'' and is the quantity that
ranks the layouts and reflectors.  $\kappa_{\rm 2D}$ is evaluated on a coarser
$5\times5$ grid of \SI{50}{\milli\meter} cells, because on the
\SI{5}{\milli\meter} cells of the maps the per-cell statistics are too thin for
a stable max/min---Poisson noise in the extreme cells would inflate it.  The two
measures therefore use different binning: the contrast quoted below is
deliberately the \emph{robust} dynamic range, and is smaller than the raw
peak-to-trough range of the fine maps.  No symmetry is assumed, so
$\kappa_{\rm 2D}$ is meaningful for the single-sided layouts~3 and~4 as well as
for the four-sided ones.

The direction-sensitive contrast $\kappa_{\rm 2D}$ ranks the reflectors
identically in \emph{every} layout,
\[
\text{TiO}_2 \;>\; \text{Al} \;>\; \text{bare} \;\gtrsim\; \text{glossy},
\]
i.e.\ the diffuse TiO$_2$ paint is the \emph{least} uniform wrapper and glossy
paper the most uniform. This runs opposite to the naive expectation that the
brightest wrappers should be the most inhomogeneous, and it follows directly
from the flat responses found above: the specular and TIR transports mix the
light so thoroughly that the entry position is largely erased
($\kappa_{\rm 2D}$ near unity), whereas the diffuse paint retains a pronounced
positional response. Numerically the TiO$_2$ contrast grows from
$\kappa_{\rm 2D} = \num{1.897}$ and \num{2.530} in the four-sided layouts~1 and~2
to \num{8.186} and \num{8.121} in the single-sided layouts~3 and~4, whereas the
bare plate stays at $\kappa_{\rm 2D} = \num{1.13}$--\num{1.49}, aluminium at
\num{1.18}--\num{1.77} and glossy at \num{1.07}--\num{1.25}. For the bare plate and the two
specular wrappers a muon crossing anywhere on the plate is therefore collected
within about a factor of two of one at the most favourable point, whereas for
TiO$_2$ in the single-sided layouts the corner-to-centre penalty approaches an
order of magnitude. Figure~\ref{fig:wholecontrast} collects all sixteen values
as bar charts, one panel per layout; Table~\ref{tab:wholerefl} lists the same
values numerically, resolving the near-unity entries that the linear bar scale
cannot.

\subsection{Arrival-time structure and pulse persistence}
\label{sec:timing}

The collection-efficiency maps above treat every detected photoelectron as a
point; the new per-photoelectron timing columns of the \texttt{sipmHits} tree
let the \emph{arrival-time structure} of the detected light be examined as well.
Figure~\ref{fig:persistence} shows the oscilloscope-style persistence map of the
arrival-time profile for the four layouts (glossy paper). For each event the
per-SiPM arrival times are summed over the layout's sensors and histogrammed in
time, giving a trace of photoelectron count versus arrival time; stacking events
and recording, for every \SI{0.1}{\nano\second} time bin, how many events showed
a given count, yields a two-dimensional map whose abscissa is the arrival time, whose
ordinate is the instantaneous photoelectron count, and whose colour is the number
of events. The rising edge and the decaying tail therefore show how the detected
light builds up and dies away, and the vertical spread at a given time is the
event-to-event fluctuation of the instantaneous rate. For the three directly-read
layouts the modal mean arrival times are \SI{3.85}{\nano\second},
\SI{3.95}{\nano\second} and \SI{3.75}{\nano\second}, and the prompt peaks reach
$54$, $55$ and $37$ photoelectrons per \SI{0.1}{\nano\second} bin in
configs~1--3; the single-fibre WLS read-out (config~4) peaks markedly later, at
\SI{9.25}{\nano\second} and at only $6$ photoelectrons per bin. All four panels

\section{Caveats and open items}
\label{sec:caveats}
% =====================================================================

\paragraph{Optical-track stall (resolved).}
During earlier matrix production runs a rare stall was observed: a single worker
thread would enter a tight loop (100\% CPU) while the remaining threads waited at
the event-level barrier, freezing the run. It was traced to insufficiently
robust photon tracking in the \emph{tessellated} config-4 fibre solid, where
near-coincident facet boundaries could leave a track stuck. The production
config-4 geometry now uses the analytic, mitered \texttt{G4CutTubs} fibre legs
(Sec.~\ref{sec:wls}) instead, whose straight, planar miter interfaces eliminate
that class of degenerate boundary; the stall has not recurred since. As a safety
net the production workflow still wraps every task in a timeout with automatic
retry.

\paragraph{The bare plate is not the weakest collector.}
Modelled with a real \SI{1.2}{\milli\meter} air gap between the polished plate
and the black holder ($f=0$), the bare configuration is a near-lossless TIR
light guide and is the \emph{second}-brightest wrapper in every layout, not the
weakest. This disagrees with Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}, whose bare plate gives by far
the smallest signal. Reproducing that suppression requires the plate--holder
interface to be optically \emph{lossy}, i.e.\ partial real contact (frustrated
TIR). The dedicated contact scan shows that a contact
fraction $f\approx\num{0.017}$ brings the bare plate below TiO$_2$ in configs~1
and~2 at all three positions and in config~3 at the centre, while the config-3
edge and corner require $f>0.03$. No single $f$ reproduces the ordering in every
layout and at every position: the required fraction is $\approx\num{0.017}$ at
the centre but only \num{0.003}--\num{0.009} at the edge/corner in
configs~1--2. We therefore keep the clean air-gap model ($f=0$) as the
production default---the defensible reading of the stated construction (a plate
simply clamped in its holder)---and report the contact fraction as an effective,
not measured, loss channel.

\paragraph{Position trend in TiO$_2$.}
For the air-gap bare plate and the mirror-like wrappers (Al, glossy) the
response is essentially flat across the three patches (bare
\SIrange{1.8}{5.6}{\percent}, Al \SIrange{4.5}{8.7}{\percent}, glossy
\SIrange{0.8}{2.9}{\percent}). The diffuse TiO$_2$ paint is the only wrapper
with a clear position dependence, and its sign depends on the layout: in the
four-sided layouts~1 and~2 it shows corner $>$ edge $>$ centre (a factor
$\approx\!\num{1.75}$), whereas in the single-sided layouts~3 and~4 it shows
centre $>$ edge $\approx$ corner. Since \texttt{groundfrontpainted} is purely
Lambertian (no TIR, no specular reflection), the sign follows the geometry of
the nearest painted walls relative to the illuminated sensors. No corresponding
trend is reported in Ref.~\cite{Rao2025} and it should be confirmed with
independent simulation settings before being interpreted physically.

\paragraph{Scale and normalisation.}
The absolute efficiencies are model-dependent: they are fixed by the assumed
PDE curve, the grease coupling, and the absorbed-fraction of the tabulated
reflectivities. The SiPM breakdown-voltage and gain (which drive the PDE) are
also temperature-dependent~\cite{Anfimov2021}, an effect not modelled here. The
robust results are the \emph{ordering} (glossy paper the brightest wrapper, and
TiO$_2$ weakest in the direct-coupled layouts) and the finding that a polished
plate in a non-contacting holder is \emph{not} suppressed.

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}
% =====================================================================

A \gfour\ optical simulation of a \SI{250}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{250}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{10}{\milli\meter} polished plastic
scintillator read out by side-mounted SiPMs has been developed and applied to a
full matrix of four SiPM layouts and four reflector treatments at three beam
positions (\num{120000} events), complemented by a uniform whole-plate
irradiation (\num{400000} events), a \num{63000}-event bare-contact scan and an
\num{18000}-event aluminium-backing scan (\num{601000} events in total). The
optical
physics---unified surface model, finish selection, photocathode PDE via the
boundary process, and per-event photon accounting---has been verified against
the \gfour\ source. The results reproduce part of the experimentally observed
ordering: glossy paper is the brightest and most uniform wrapper in every
layout, the diffuse TiO$_2$ paint is the most position-dependent and the weakest
wrapper in the three direct-coupled layouts (the aluminium foil in the WLS
layout). They do \emph{not}, however, reproduce the experimental result that
the bare plate is by far the weakest collector. Modelled with its true air gap
($f=0$), the polished plate is a near-lossless TIR light guide, second only to
glossy paper in every layout. The contact scan shows that an effective contact
fraction $f\approx\num{0.017}$ at the centre ($0.003$--\num{0.009} at the
edge/corner) suppresses the bare plate below TiO$_2$ in configs~1 and~2 at all
three positions and in config~3 at the centre, but config~3 edge/corner require
$f>0.03$; no single $f$ matches every layout and position. An air-backed aluminium model (backing index \num{1.0003}) raises
the foil to $\approx\SI{92}{\percent}$ of the glossy yield, recovering the
measured near-equality of the two best wrappers, while the production whole-slab
runs use the grease-backed default. Keeping the three irradiation patches
separate---rather than collapsing them to a single beam-axis
radius---shows that the bare plate and the reflective wrappers homogenise the
response over the illuminated region (position contrast
\SIrange{1.8}{5.6}{\percent} for the bare plate, \SIrange{4.5}{8.7}{\percent}
for Al and \SIrange{0.8}{2.9}{\percent} for glossy), while only the diffuse
TiO$_2$ paint retains a clear position trend (\SIrange{75}{84}{\percent}).
Extending the scan to the whole plate sharpens this
picture: the full-plate contrast is largest for TiO$_2$
($\kappa_{\rm 2D} = \num{1.90}$--\num{8.19}), reaches only \num{1.13}--\num{1.49}
for the bare plate, and stays below about \num{1.8} for the specular wrappers.
