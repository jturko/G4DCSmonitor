\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We describe a \gfour\ (v11.3.2) simulation of a
\SI{250}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{250}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{10}{\milli\meter} polished
plastic scintillator (PVT, UPS-92S/EJ-200 class) read out by side-coupled
silicon photomultipliers (SiPMs), modelled on the muon-trigger detector of
Rao \emph{et al.}\ [J.~Appl.\ Phys.\ \textbf{138}, 114504 (2025)], and ask how
far it reproduces the \emph{measured} light-collection behaviour of four
surface treatments.

The physics question is sharper than it first appears, because the four
treatments differ in \emph{how} they return light to the plate, not in how
much they return. A polished plate facing a black holder is a near-lossless
total-internal-reflection light guide and ought to be the brightest
collector; in the measurement it is the dimmest. We resolve that inversion
with two calibrated surface parameters---a mean-field plate--holder contact
fraction $f$ and the optical backing of the aluminium foil---and then show
that the measured ordering is recovered for every directly-coupled layout.

With $f=\num{0.045}$ and an air-backed foil, a \num{48}-file matrix scan
(4 SiPM layouts $\times$ 4 treatments $\times$ 3 beam positions,
\num{2500} events each) and a whole-plate irradiation (\num{25000} events per
configuration and treatment) give, for the three directly-coupled layouts,
\[
  \text{bare} < \text{TiO}_2 < \text{Al} < \text{glossy},
\]
with glossy paper brightest, air-backed aluminium within
\SI{92}{\percent} of it, and the contact-limited bare plate weakest. The
WLS-fibre layout keeps a different ordering
($\text{TiO}_2<\text{bare}<\text{Al}<\text{glossy}$), because the black
holder is absent there. The ordering is explained by a single mechanism:
the response tracks the \emph{distance from the interaction point to the
nearest sensor} whenever the cavity is not a perfect light mixer, so the
diffuse paint (contrast $\kappa_{\rm 2D}=\num{1.91}$--\num{8.31}) and the
contact-limited bare plate ($\num{1.36}$--$\num{3.82}$) are position
dependent, while the specular wrappers ($\num{1.07}$--\num{1.28}) are not.
That same mechanism is what makes the eight channels of config~1 a position
detector: with the diffuse paint the light sharing alone localises the muon
to \SI{12.5}{\milli\meter} per axis, whereas the specular wrappers carry the
information in the arrival-time pattern instead and need a fast time
digitisation to reach \SI{19}{\milli\meter}. We also report per-event photon
yields, photoelectron spectra, wavelength and arrival-time distributions, and
a systematic comparison with the published measurements, including the one
wrapper for which the simulation and the measurement disagree on the sign of
the position trend.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and motivation}
% =====================================================================

Plastic scintillators read out at their edges by SiPMs are attractive for
large-area, low-cost muon triggers and veto systems for cosmic-ray muon
tomography~\cite{Borozdin2003,Bonechi2020}. The amount of scintillation
light that reaches the photosensor is governed almost entirely by how the
non-instrumented faces of the plate are treated: a polished plate in air traps
light by total internal reflection (TIR) and can be a nearly lossless light
guide, whereas a plate wrapped in an absorbing or diffusive material loses
photons at every bounce. Understanding this trade-off quantitatively is the
central design question, and it is precisely where simulation is most useful,
because the photon transport is a multi-bounce random walk that is difficult
to model analytically~\cite{Korpachev2017}.

The naive expectation, however, is the opposite of what the experiment finds,
and the reason is not obvious. Of the four surface treatments compared in
Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}---no reflective layer at all, a white TiO$_2$ paint,
aluminium foil and high-gloss paper---the one that \emph{loses} the least
light optically is the bare plate in its black holder: it is a polished
TIR guide separated from the holder by a real air gap, whereas the paint
absorbs or diffusely scatters at every bounce and the two mirrors lose
\SI{8}{\percent}--\SI{10}{\percent} of the light that reaches them. On that
reasoning alone the bare plate should collect \emph{most} of the light. In
the measurement it collects the \emph{least}: a clamped plate touches its
holder at scattered high spots, and each contact frustrates the total
internal reflection that the whole design relies on. Reproducing the
measurement therefore requires modelling not only the reflectors but also
the \emph{non}-reflector, and the size of that hidden loss channel has to be
calibrated against data.

This report documents a \gfour\ (v11.3.2)~\cite{Agostinelli2003,Allison2016}
simulation built to address that question for the specific geometry and
surface treatments of Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}. The deliverable is a
self-contained ``matrix scan'': every combination of four SiPM layouts and
four reflector treatments, at three beam positions on the plate, plus a
uniform whole-plate irradiation. The goal is not to fit a single number but
to test how far the simulation reproduces the \emph{ordering} and the
\emph{position dependence} of the light-collection efficiency that the
experiment observes, to expose the physics behind both the parts it
reproduces and the parts it does not, and to quantify the residuals.

Table~\ref{tab:glance} collects the whole study in one place, so that the

\section{The measurement being reproduced}
\label{sec:measurement}
% =====================================================================

Before presenting results it is worth stating what is being reproduced, in
the terms of the experiment, and where the model and the experiment are
known to differ. Everything in this section is quoted from
Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}.

\subsection{What was measured}

A \SI{250}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{250}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{10}{\milli\meter}
plate of UPS-92S organic scintillator is read out by
CEFLA S.C.\ ASD-NUV4S-P-40 SiPMs, each with a \SI{4}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{4}{\milli\meter}
active area, mounted on the \emph{narrow} side faces of the plate. That
choice is deliberate: because the area of the narrow faces is small, a large
fraction of the light is transported by total internal reflection off the
large faces and then collected towards the narrow ones, so the detected yield
is higher on the narrow faces than on the large ones. Configurations with
the SiPM and the fibre clamp on the large faces were explicitly not
considered, for the same reason.

Four \emph{surface treatments} were applied to the non-instrumented faces,
in addition to the bare plate:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item EJ-510 white reflector paint with TiO$_2$ suspension (Eljen
        Technology);
  \item commercial aluminium foil, shiny side out, with small amounts of
        optical grease applied selectively to ensure contact between the
        scintillator and the reflective layer;
  \item no reflective material at all --- the polished plate simply
        \emph{placed} in a black 3D-printed holder;
  \item super high-gloss resin-coated photo paper (\SI{300}{\gram\per\square\meter},
        Print4Life), cut to size and placed on each surface \emph{without}
        grease.
\end{enumerate}
These are the four treatments modelled here, one-to-one.

Four \emph{read-out configurations} were then assembled (Table~IV of
Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}), and are the four layouts of this report:
\begin{description}
  \item[Config 1] eight SiPMs, two on each of the four narrow faces
        (\texttt{TBLR1}, \texttt{TBLR2}), \SI{128}{\milli\meter\squared} of
        active area in total;
  \item[Config 2] four SiPMs, one on each narrow face (\texttt{TBLR1}),
        \SI{64}{\milli\meter\squared};
  \item[Config 3] two SiPMs, both on the same narrow face (\texttt{T1},
        \texttt{T2}), \SI{32}{\milli\meter\squared};
  \item[Config 4] one SiPM plus a wavelength-shifting fibre clamp, whose
        four fibres provide an effective collection area of
        \SI{2500}{\milli\meter\squared}.
\end{description}

The efficiency was measured with a muon coincidence detector (MCD) system of
two \SI{50}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{50}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{10}{\milli\meter}
reflective-painted reference scintillators read out by photomultiplier
tubes. The MCD was placed over one of three \emph{regions of interest} on
the plate --- the central region, a middle-edge region and a corner region
--- and coincidences between the two MCD arms provided the reference muon
sample. The reported observable is the detection efficiency
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{DE}(u_{\mathrm{thresh}})
  = \frac{N_{\mathrm{det}}(u_{\mathrm{thresh}})}{N_{\mathrm{ref}}},
  \label{eq:de}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the fraction of reference muons that produce a trigger signal above the
discriminator threshold $u_{\mathrm{thresh}}$, together with the pulse-height
(detected photon yield) distribution of the trigger itself.

\subsection{The statements this simulation is tested against}

The experiment reports a small number of clear, qualitative statements, and
these are the yardstick used in Sec.~\ref{sec:comparison}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item configurations \emph{without} a reflective layer consistently
        produce the lowest detected photon yield at the SiPMs;
  \item the signal-to-noise ratios are highest and nearly identical for the
        aluminium foil and the high-gloss paper;
  \item the lowest detected photon yield distributions occur for all
        configurations when the MCD is positioned at the \emph{corner} of
        the scintillator plate;
  \item all configurations except those without a reflective layer reach a
        detection efficiency of \SI{80}{\percent} or more at
        $u_{\mathrm{thresh}} = \SI{400}{\milli\volt}$, whereas the bare
        configuration is the only one that falls short;
  \item the WLS-fibre configuration (config~4) produces a significantly
        higher detected photon yield distribution than config~3 at all
        measurement positions, and reaches a detection efficiency of
        \SI{90}{\percent} at $u_{\mathrm{thresh}} = \SI{400}{\milli\volt}$
        across its full active area;
  \item the converted (wavelength-shifted) photons still produce electrical
        signals with approximately \SI{30}{\percent} efficiency.
\end{itemize}
The first three concern the \emph{ordering} of the treatments and the
\emph{sign} of the position trend, and are the statements this simulation
reproduces, fails to reproduce, or contradicts; the fourth and fifth are
threshold statements that the simulation can address only indirectly, since
it contains no electronics (Sec.~\ref{sec:caveats}); the sixth quantifies
the optical efficiency of the WLS path and is the natural yardstick for the
one configuration that the simulation does \emph{not} reproduce.

\subsection{Where the model deliberately departs from the experiment}

Two geometric departures are worth stating explicitly, because both affect
the comparison and neither is accidental.

\emph{The config-3 and config-4 faces.} In the experiment, config~3 reads out
the \emph{top} narrow face and the config-4 fibre clamp sits on the
\emph{left} narrow face --- two adjacent, not shared, faces. In this
simulation both assemblies are placed on the same narrow face of the model
(Sec.~\ref{sec:detector}), which has the advantage of keeping the four
layouts on a common geometric footing and of making the two single-sided
layouts directly comparable to each other, at the cost of removing the
adjacency that the experiment realises. Any statement about the relative
performance of config~3 and config~4 in this report therefore refers to the
shared-face geometry and cannot be read as a statement about the
experimental pair.

\emph{The position of the irradiated patches.} The three regions of interest
are defined by where the MCD is placed, and in the experiment they are
chosen so that the MCD (which is \SI{50}{\milli\meter} square) sits entirely
on the plate. Here the same requirement is imposed, and the edge and corner
patches are placed on the side of the plate \emph{opposite} the instrumented
face of the single-sided layouts, so that the three patches sample the far
side of the cavity for configs~3 and~4.

Finally, the experiment itself warns that the four configurations
``cannot be directly compared with one another'', because the number of
parallel SiPMs changes the total input capacitance, the effective gain and
the mean probability of a photon hitting the sensitive area. The same
caveat applies here, and it is the reason the results below are presented
per layout rather than pooled.

\section{The detector and its optical model}
\label{sec:detector}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Scintillator plate}

The active volume is a rectangular PVT scintillator
$250 \times 250 \times \SI{10}{\milli\meter}$, mirroring the UPS-92S plate of
Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}. It is assigned the \gfour\ NIST base material
\texttt{G4\_PLASTIC\_SC\_VINYLTOLUENE} with a custom optical properties table
built from the EJ-200 and UPS-923A data
sheets~\cite{EljenEJ200,Artikov2005,AmcrysPS}. The optical constants---the
refractive index ($n = 1.58$, essentially flat across the visible), light yield,
emission peak, bulk attenuation length, scintillation decay and rise times, and
the Birks constant~\cite{Birks1951,Craun1970}---are collected in
Table~\ref{tab:detparams} and are not repeated here. Two entries are
deliberate modelling choices rather than quoted numbers: the bulk attenuation
length is peaked at \SI{3.8}{\meter} but falls steeply in the UV to model
self-absorption of the short-wavelength part of the emission, and the emission
spectrum (Fig.~\ref{fig:spectra_opt}) is essentially zero below
\SI{380}{\nano\meter} and above \SI{550}{\nano\meter}. Both scintillation
and \v{C}erenkov photons are produced and tracked; the optical grid spans
\SIrange{310}{700}{\nano\meter} so that the \v{C}erenkov continuum, which
extends well below the scintillation band, is covered.

\subsection{SiPM readout and coupling}

Each SiPM~\cite{Gundacker2020} is modelled mechanically as a
$4 \times 4 \times \SI{0.6}{\milli\meter}$ box of ``SiPM epoxy'' ($n = 1.55$),
coupled to a side face of the plate through a
\SI{0.1}{\milli\meter} silicone grease pad ($n = 1.46$). The coupling chain is
therefore \emph{scintillator\,$\to$\,grease\,$\to$\,SiPM}, with Fresnel
transmission at each dielectric interface and photodetection at the final
grease/SiPM boundary.

Four layouts are studied (schematic in Fig.~\ref{fig:layouts}):
\begin{description}
  \item[Config 1 (8 SiPMs)] two SiPMs per side face, at $\pm 50\%$ of the half-length.
  \item[Config 2 (4 SiPMs)] one SiPM per side face, at the \emph{same} $\pm 50\%$
        offsets as config~1---i.e.\ config~1 with one device removed per side,
        the removed slot rotated from side to side so the layout remains
        four-fold symmetric.
  \item[Config 3 (2 SiPMs)] both SiPMs on the \emph{same} narrow
        face---the $-x$ face in the simulation frame, which is the report
        plan view's top edge (Sec.~\ref{sec:sim})---at $\pm 50\%$, matching
        T1/T2 of Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}.
  \item[Config 4 (1 SiPM $+$ WLS clamp)] a single SiPM reading out a
        wavelength-shifting (WLS) fibre clamp along the $-x$ narrow face
        (Sec.~\ref{sec:wls}).
\end{description}
SiPM footprints are clamped so they cannot overhang the slab face.

\subsection{Reflector treatments}
\label{sec:reflectors}

surfaces of the plate; the grease/SiPM patches override the skin locally with
dedicated \emph{border} surfaces. The mapping from each physical treatment to the
\gfour\ optical-surface parameters is the heart of the model---it determines
the light-collection ordering---so it is given in full. Table~\ref{tab:surfaces}
lists every property that is actually attached to each surface, and the
subsubsections below describe each reflector physically, state how it is
modelled, and assess how faithfully the model represents the real object. (A
fifth finish, PTFE tape, exists in the code for completeness but is not part of
the reference study.) The strong sensitivity of light collection to the wrapping
and reflector choice is well established
experimentally~\cite{Taheri2017}.

\subsubsection{Bare plate in its black holder}
\label{sec:refl-bare}

\emph{Physical object.} The polished UPS-92S plate is placed in a black
3D-printed ABS enclosure, its faces simply resting near the wall.

\emph{Model.} No wrapping skin is attached. The plate keeps its native polished
scintillator ($n=1.58$) against air ($n=1.0003$) across a real
\SI{1.2}{\milli\meter} gap; light below the critical angle
($\arcsin(1/1.58)=\SI{39.3}{\degree}$) refracts across the gap and is absorbed in
the holder, whose black ABS has an attenuation length of
\SI{1}{\micro\meter}, while light above it is returned by total internal
reflection. Fresnel reflection and TIR are taken from the material
\texttt{RINDEX} tables.

\emph{Fidelity.} This is the least faithful configuration and the source of the
report's central modelling choice. A real clamped plate touches the holder at
scattered high spots, and each contact forms a frustrated-TIR sink that removes
trapped light; surface scratches do the same. Those loss channels are absent in
the clean air-gap model ($f=0$), where the plate acts as a near-lossless light
guide and is one of the \emph{brightest} collectors, whereas the reference
measurement gives it the \emph{lowest} signal. The code therefore offers an
optional mean-field contact fraction,
\texttt{/dcs-monitor/det/muonScint/setBareContactFraction <f>}
($0\le f\le 1$): for $f>0$ every hit on a bare face is absorbed with probability
$f$ (a polished \texttt{dielectric\_dielectric} skin with
\texttt{REFLECTIVITY} $=1-f$ and \texttt{TRANSMITTANCE} $=0$), representing
frustrated TIR over an effective contact area. The loss is ignored for the
config-4 bare layout, whose clamp occupies that face. The dedicated scan
(Sec.~\ref{sec:contact}) gives a nominal
$f=\num{0.045}$, the smallest sampled fraction that places all nine
config/position cells below the corresponding TiO$_2$ reference; $f=0$ is kept
as the clean-air reference and lower bound on the loss.

\subsubsection{Titanium-dioxide paint (EJ-510)}
\label{sec:refl-tio2}

\emph{Physical object.} A white, diffuse-reflectance paint applied directly to
the plate (three coats, $\approx\SI{0.11}{\milli\meter}$), in optical contact
with the scintillator.

\emph{Model.} \texttt{groundfrontpainted} with a purely Lambertian reflection: a
random draw against \texttt{REFLECTIVITY} returns the photon with a cosine-law
distribution and \emph{no} specular or TIR component
($f_{ss}=f_{sl}=f_{bs}=0$). The reflectance rises from \num{0.955} in the UV to
a plateau of \num{0.983} near \SI{470}{\nano\meter}.

\emph{Fidelity.} The Lambertian/no-TIR treatment is the physically correct
description of light emerging from a thick, strongly scattering paint, and is
this wrapper's strongest feature. The \emph{absolute} reflectance curve is its
weakest: no public spectral-reflectance data exist for EJ-510, so $R(\lambda)$
is a fitted representative white-paint curve ($\approx 0.96$--$0.98$), not a
measurement of the actual coating, and no angular dependence of the diffuse
lobe is modelled. The \texttt{sigma\_alpha} value is set for bookkeeping but is
inert at this finish (\texttt{groundfrontpainted} reflects diffusely regardless
of the facet normal).

\subsubsection{Aluminium foil}
\label{sec:refl-al}

\emph{Physical object.} Commercial shiny aluminium foil wrapped around the
plate; per Ref.~\cite{Rao2025} optical grease is applied in places so that the
foil contacts the scintillator rather than leaving an air gap.

\emph{Model.} \texttt{polishedbackpainted} with a selectable backing refractive
index, chosen pre-init with
\texttt{/dcs-monitor/det/muonScint/setAlBacking air|grease}. The production
choice is \texttt{air} ($n=1.0003$), which leaves the full TIR cone intact and
lets only the sub-critical fraction cross to the foil, modelling a foil
separated from the plate by a trapped air layer. The alternative,
\texttt{grease} ($n=1.46$), represents a foil applied over the coupling grease,
as in Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}; the scintillator/grease interface is a normal
Fresnel/TIR boundary, but since the index step is small the critical angle is
wide ($\arcsin(1.46/1.58)=\SI{67.5}{\degree}$): most light refracts into the
grease film and is returned by the foil as a pure \emph{specular} reflection
($f_{ss}=1$). It is retained as the lower-bound systematic. In both cases
\texttt{REFLECTIVITY} is the normal-incidence reflectance of evaporated
aluminium computed from the McPeak \emph{et al.}~$n,k$
data~\cite{McPeak2015} (\num{0.904} at \SI{650}{\nano\meter} rising to
\num{0.925} at \SI{360}{\nano\meter}).

\emph{Fidelity.} Two idealisations. (i) The tabulated reflectance is that of a
smooth evaporated film and is therefore an \emph{upper bound}: household foil is
rougher and carries a native oxide, both of which lower $R$ and add a small
diffuse component we do not model. (ii) The foil is assumed to cover the plate
uniformly, whereas real wrapping leaves wrinkles and trapped air. The production
campaign uses the \texttt{air} backing, which retains the full TIR cone and
recovers the measured near-equality of aluminium and glossy paper, reaching
$\approx\SI{92}{\percent}$ of the glossy yield in the direct-coupled layouts.
The \texttt{grease} backing is retained as the lower-bound systematic
(Sec.~\ref{sec:albacking}). Note that the air backing is itself the
\emph{optimistic} end of the physical range: it is the limiting case of a
perfectly trapped air layer, whereas the experiment describes grease applied
\emph{selectively}, i.e.\ a foil that contacts the plate in places and is
decoupled in others. Neither extreme is the real object, and the scan of
Sec.~\ref{sec:albacking} brackets them.

\subsubsection{High-gloss photo paper}
\label{sec:refl-glossy}

\emph{Physical object.} A commercial super-high-gloss photographic paper
(Print4Life, \SI{300}{\gram\per\square\meter}) wrapped around the plate with an
air gap (no grease).

\emph{Model.} \texttt{polishedbackpainted} with the backing index set to air
($n=1.0003$), so the plate retains its full TIR cone and only the sub-critical
fraction crosses the gap to the paper, which returns it as a specular reflection
($f_{ss}=1$) with a fitted $R(\lambda)\approx 0.900$--$0.955$---slightly lossier
than the aluminium.

\emph{Fidelity.} The reference reports glossy paper and aluminium foil as the two
best wrappers (large, nearly equal signals), which motivates modelling glossy
paper as a \emph{slightly lossy mirror} rather than a diffuser. This is a model
choice rather than a measurement: real photo paper is not a true mirror and has
a diffuse component, and its $R(\lambda)$ is again fitted (no measured data),
so this is the reflector whose optical model is least constrained by independent
data.

\subsection{Config 4: wavelength-shifting fibre clamp}
\label{sec:wls}

The fourth layout follows the single-SiPM, WLS-readout variant of
Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}. Four \SI{1.0}{\milli\meter}-diameter Kuraray Y-11(200)
multi-clad wavelength-shifting fibres~\cite{KurarayPSF,Alekseev2022} run the full
\SI{250}{\milli\meter} length of the $-x$ narrow face in a single row. Over most
of the length they are parallel at a \SI{2.5}{\milli\meter} centre-to-centre
pitch---so a solid web of clamp material separates the milled
\SI{1.1}{\milli\meter} channels---and converge over a \SI{41}{\milli\meter}
length (\SI{39}{\milli\meter} ramp plus a \SI{2}{\milli\meter} stub) to a
touching \SI{1.0}{\milli\meter} bundle that presents
all four ends to a single \SI{4}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{4}{\milli\meter} SiPM at
the $-y$ end; their far $+y$ ends are closed by individual aluminium mirror caps.
Each fibre is modelled as a multi-clad guide: a Y-11 core ($n = 1.59$) inside a
PMMA inner cladding ($n = 1.49$) inside a low-index fluoropolymer outer sheath
($n = 1.42$)---the low-index staircase that sustains core--cladding total
internal reflection. In the production geometry the fibre solid is an analytic,
mitered set of \texttt{G4CutTubs} legs (one per straight segment of the bent
centreline), placed so adjoining legs meet at exact miter planes; this
facet-free construction is what the runs use. (An earlier tessellated swept
cylinder, which occasionally produced stuck optical tracks, is retained as an
optional visualisation-only variant.) A thin \SI{0.10}{\milli\meter} grease film
couples the plate face to the clamp, and each fibre protrudes
\SI{0.05}{\milli\meter} through its channel mouth into that film, keeping the
grease continuous against the scintillator. The deliberate
\SI{0.05}{\milli\meter} air annulus between the \SI{1.0}{\milli\meter} fibre and
its \SI{1.1}{\milli\meter} channel is essential: the fibre must not touch the
clamp ($n = 1.55$), and the outer sheath ($n = 1.42$) needs the low-index
clearance to keep guiding the light. The clamp body is 3D-printed white plastic,
modelled by default as a Lambertian reflector with \SI{85}{\percent} reflectivity
applied to \emph{every} clamp face (channel walls and grease-facing front
included), so light leaking out of the fibre is returned rather than lost. The
far-end mirror is a full aluminium disc whose logical volume carries a reflecting
skin as well as the fibre--cap border surfaces, so that light travelling in the
\emph{core}---not only the cladding---is also turned around at the far end. WLS
re-emission is drawn from the digitized Y-11 emission spectrum with an
exponential delay of \SI{7}{\nano\second} (Sec.~\ref{sec:wlphysics}), and the
\texttt{G4OpWLS} process is enabled automatically when this geometry is built. In
the report's top-down frame the clamp runs along the \emph{top} edge with the
single read-out SiPM at the \emph{top-left} corner, as in Fig.~3(b) of
Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}.

\subsection{Optical physics implementation}
\label{sec:physics}

Optical photons are transported by the standard \gfour\ optical physics
module~\cite{Geant4PRM}, with scintillation, \v{C}erenkov, bulk
absorption, Rayleigh scattering and boundary processes all active. Multiple
scattering (Mie) is disabled; wavelength shifting (the \texttt{G4OpWLS} process)
is enabled only for the config-4 fibre layout (Sec.~\ref{sec:wls}) and inert for
all others. The boundary physics of the \gfour\ unified surface model, the
\texttt{RINDEX} requirements it imposes, and the three code paths used by the
four finishes are described in Appendix~\ref{app:g4}, where they are verified
line-by-line against the \gfour\ source. This subsection states the two
process-specific pieces of the model: the wavelength shifter and the
photocathode.

\subsection{Wavelength shifting}
\label{sec:wlphysics}

In the config-4 layout, photons that reach the fibre cores are absorbed and
re-emitted isotropically at longer wavelength by the \texttt{G4OpWLS} process,
which is configured through the fibre material's \texttt{WLSABSLENGTH},
\texttt{WLSCOMPONENT} and \texttt{WLSTIMECONSTANT} properties. Both spectra are
the digitized Kuraray Y-11(200) curves: the absorption band peaks at
\SI{430}{\nano\meter} (onset $\approx\SI{486}{\nano\meter}$) and the re-emission
peak is \SI{476}{\nano\meter} (Fig.~\ref{fig:spectra_opt}). The absolute
absorption strength is set by the \SI{430}{\nano\meter} absorption length,
\SI{1.0}{\milli\meter} in production (with a systematic band of
\SIrange{0.3}{3}{\milli\meter} available through the
\texttt{setWLSAbsLength} command, but not scanned here; see
Sec.~\ref{sec:caveats}). The re-emission is sampled with an
exponential time profile, $\tau = \SI{7}{\nano\second}$, rather than a fixed
delay. \texttt{G4OpWLS} samples the secondary from \texttt{WLSCOMPONENT} subject
to $E_\gamma' \le E_\gamma$, so the emission table is cut at the absorption
onset; a small overlap of the emission tail with the absorption band gives mild
self-absorption. The process is switched on by the physics list when the WLS
geometry is detected (the \gfour\ optical-physics constructor reads the process
activation at build time, so the flag is set before the base construction).
Because most of the shifted emission lies red-ward of the absorption band, the
re-emitted light is transported along the fibre by TIR to the read-out
SiPM---which is why the detected config-4 spectrum is green-shifted (mean
$\sim\!\SI{510}{\nano\meter}$) relative to the direct-coupled configurations.

\subsection{Photodetection: the SiPM photocathode}

Photodetection is delegated entirely to \gfour, rather than being drawn
manually in the sensitive detector. The grease/SiPM boundary is a
\texttt{dielectric\_metal} surface whose MPT carries the SiPM
\texttt{EFFICIENCY} as a function of photon energy (equivalently, of the
wavelength $\lambda$), i.e.\ the photon detection
efficiency (PDE) of an ASD-NUV4S-P-40 device~\cite{AdvanSiD_NUV}, peaking at
$0.431$ near \SI{410}{\nano\meter} (the digitized 25-point curve of
Fig.~\ref{fig:spectra_opt}; values fall to $\approx 0.30$ at
\SI{490}{\nano\meter}), with \texttt{REFLECTIVITY} $=0$.

The mechanism is worth stating precisely because it is the reason the sensitive
detector needs no random draw of its own. In \texttt{DielectricMetal()},
when the Fresnel--PDE roll places a photon in the ``absorbed''
channel, \texttt{DoAbsorption()} is called. Inside it:
\begin{equation}
  f_{\mathrm{status}} =
  \begin{cases}
    \mathrm{Absorption}, & \xi \ge \operatorname{EFFICIENCY}(\lambda),\\[2pt]
    \mathrm{Detection},  & \xi <  \operatorname{EFFICIENCY}(\lambda),
  \end{cases}
  \qquad \xi \sim U(0,1),
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{EFFICIENCY}(\lambda)$ is the tabulated PDE. The absorbed
photon is assigned a non-zero
\texttt{LocalEnergyDeposit} \emph{only} when the PDE draw succeeds. Back in
\texttt{PostStepDoIt}, if the status is \texttt{Detection} \emph{and} the
invoke-SD flag is set, the sensitive detector is invoked:
\begin{equation}
  f_{\mathrm{status}} = \mathrm{Detection}\ \wedge\ f_{\mathrm{InvokeSD}}
  \ \Longrightarrow\ \operatorname{InvokeSD}(\text{step}).
\end{equation}
The flag is enabled in the physics list via
\texttt{G4OpticalParameters::SetBoundaryInvokeSD(true)}. Consequently the
sensitive-detector \texttt{ProcessHits} fires \emph{only} for genuine
photoelectrons (it tests that the step energy deposit is positive), counts one
photoelectron per call, and kills the photon (the SiPM is opaque). This is a clean
separation of concerns: the physics of the photocathode lives in the optical
surface, and the sensitive detector only books hits.

\subsection{Per-event optical accounting}

Two per-event counts recur throughout this report. Write $\pe^{(e,s)}$ for the
number of photoelectrons detected in SiPM $s$ during event $e$, and
$\ngam^{(e)}$ for the number of optical photons generated in the slab during
event $e$ (scintillation plus \v{C}erenkov), irrespective of their later fate.

A thread-local diagnostic record (\texttt{OpticalDiag}) accumulates, per event:
scintillation photons, \v{C}erenkov photons, terminated photons, detected
photoelectrons, and photons that escaped to the world. The primary muon's
first-entry position in the slab is captured on the first step inside the
scintillator logical volume and stored in slab-local coordinates. At end of
event the sensitive detector writes one row per (event, SiPM), \emph{including
zero-detection rows}, so the sample is unbiased in the muon interaction
position. Each row carries the entry position, the event's produced-photon
count (the efficiency denominator), the detected count in that SiPM, the first
arrival time, the mean and RMS detected wavelength, and the track weight. Three
variable-length branches additionally list the individual photoelectrons: their
global arrival times (\texttt{tDetected}), wavelengths
(\texttt{wlDetected\_nm}) and creating processes (\texttt{creatorDetected}, with
$1 =$ \texttt{OpWLS}, $2 =$ scintillation, $3 =$ \v{C}erenkov). The per-row
count, timing and spectral data therefore support per-SiPM, combined and
spatially-binned collection-efficiency analyses together with offline
pulse-shape and process-mix studies.

Figure~\ref{fig:vis} shows a head-less event display of a single vertical muon
in the glossy-wrapped plate (config~1). For legibility the display uses a
\SI{30}{\mega\electronvolt} muon---just below the \v{C}erenkov threshold of the
scintillator---with the scintillation yield rescaled, so that the individual
optical tracks can be followed: photons are generated along the muon path,
propagate by total internal reflection and specular reflection off the wrapper,
and terminate on the SiPM patches at the four edges.

\subsection{Beam, irradiation patches, and efficiency definition}

The primary source is a vertical muon pencil beam: a \SI{50}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{50}{\milli\meter}
square beam of \SI{4}{\giga\electronvolt} $\mu^-$ normally incident on the plate,
with the beam centre placed at three positions, each irradiating a
\SI{50}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{50}{\milli\meter} patch lying \emph{entirely
on} the polished plate. With the plate spanning $[-125,125]\,\si{\milli\meter}$
in both $x$ and $y$, the patch centres are
\[
  \text{centre} = (0,0),\qquad
  \text{edge} = (0,-100),\qquad
  \text{corner} = (100,-100)\ \si{\milli\meter},
\]
so the illuminated regions are $[-25,25]^2$, $[-25,25]\times[-125,-75]$ and
$[75,125]\times[-125,-75]$ respectively (report frame: $+y$ is the top,
$-x$ the left). This places the edge and corner regions on the bottom
($-y$) edge, opposite the single instrumented face of config~3 (top, $+y$),
and it matches the \SI{50}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{50}{\milli\meter}
active area of the muon coincidence detector (MCD) that defines the regions of
interest in Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}. All three patches have the same area, so the
denominator of Eq.~\ref{eq:eff} is the same at all three positions and the
three points of a curve are directly comparable. Each
(config, reflector, position) runs \num{2500} events after a single
\texttt{/run/initialize}. The matrix therefore comprises
\[
  4\ \text{configs} \times 4\ \text{reflectors} \times 3\ \text{positions}
  = 48\ \text{files}, \qquad 48 \times \num{2500} = \num{120000}\ \text{events}.
\]
Two focused calibration campaigns bracket the remaining surface inputs and are
presented first. A bare-contact scan
runs configs~1--3 at eleven contact fractions
$f=0$, $0.005$, $\dots$, $0.050$ at the three beam positions,
$3 \times 11 \times 3 = \num{99}$ files with \num{1000} events each
(\num{99000} events); an aluminium-backing scan runs configs~1--3 with
\texttt{grease} and \texttt{air} backing at the three positions,
$3 \times 2 \times 3 = \num{18}$ files with \num{1000} events each
(\num{18000} events). The Al scan is taken from the earlier production
campaign because the Al model is unchanged and that scan is complete; the
contact scan is the new extended campaign. Together with the whole-plate run of
Sec.~\ref{sec:yield} ($16 \times \num{25000} = \num{400000}$ events), the
campaign totals \num{637000} events, of which \num{237000} are matrix-style
($\text{matrix} + \text{contact} + \text{Al}$).

Production runs were executed on the HPC cluster.
The optional per-slab optical track-length flux map is disabled for the matrix
(it is retained as a separate diagnostic); the collection efficiency is
reconstructed entirely offline from the \texttt{sipmHits} tree at any desired
binning, so the map is not needed.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.46\textwidth]{figures/irradiation_sites.pdf}

\label{fig:irr}

The collection efficiency is defined, for a set $\mathcal{S}$ of SiPMs and an
event $e$, as
\begin{equation}
  \varepsilon = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{e}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}} \pe^{(e,s)}}
                     {\displaystyle\sum_{e}\ngam^{(e)}},
  \label{eq:eff}
\end{equation}
where the outer sum runs over all simulated events and the inner sum over the
SiPM set $\mathcal{S}$. The all-SiPM sum over the three beam
positions is the per-position figure of merit. A \emph{plate-averaged}
efficiency is the appropriate global figure of merit, but the three patches
sample only part of the plate; it is therefore obtained from the uniform
whole-plate irradiation of Sec.~\ref{sec:yield} and reported there.

For presentation, all position-resolved results (the maps of
Fig.~\ref{fig:irr} and the whole-slab maps
of Sec.~\ref{sec:uniformity}) are shown in the report frame with the top edge at $+y$,
the bottom at $-y$, the left at $-x$ and the right at $+x$. The internal
simulation frame is this view rotated by \SI{90}{\degree} clockwise; the primary
muon entry points and the SiPM markers are mapped into the report frame by
$(x,y)\to(y,-x)$ before plotting. This rotation is a rigid change of view and
leaves every scalar result (yields, spectra, efficiencies) unchanged; it only
fixes the orientation of config~3 (top edge) and of the edge/corner irradiation
sites (bottom edge).

The optical measurements do not fix two remaining surface inputs: the effective
bare-holder contact fraction $f$ and the aluminium backing. We determine both
before presenting any production result, so that every matrix and whole-slab
yield below uses one consistent nominal surface model. Both calibrations use the
same beam patches and efficiency definition just described.

\subsection{Bare-plate holder contact}
\label{sec:contact}

The clean bare model assumes a \SI{1.2}{\milli\meter} air gap between the
polished plate and the black holder. A real clamped plate touches the holder at
scattered high spots, and each contact frustrates total internal reflection and
removes trapped light. To bracket this unmeasured loss channel we repeat the
three-patch scan for a mean-field contact fraction $f$: every hit on a bare face
is absorbed with probability $f$ and otherwise follows the normal Fresnel/TIR
path, implemented as a polished \texttt{dielectric\_dielectric} skin with
\texttt{REFLECTIVITY} $=1-f$ and \texttt{TRANSMITTANCE} $=0$
(Sec.~\ref{sec:refl-bare}). The extended scan covers configs~1--3, the three beam
positions and eleven fractions $f=0$, $0.005$, $\dots$, $0.050$, with \num{1000}
events per case ($99$ files, \num{99000} events). It is an effective area
fraction, not a measurement of the holder gap.

Figure~\ref{fig:contact} shows the absolute yield versus $f$, with the matrix
TiO$_2$ reference drawn as dashed lines, and the normalised centre yield.
At $f=0$ the config-1 bare plate collects \num{304.95}, \num{295.37} and
\num{288.55}~\pe/event at the centre, edge and corner; the corresponding config-2
values are \num{160.21}, \num{153.68} and \num{152.25}, and the config-3 values
\num{81.27}, \num{79.20} and \num{80.23}. A 1\% contact fraction already removes
about half the light: at the config-1 centre the yield falls to \num{155.01}
\pe/event, and at the nominal $f=\num{0.045}$ to \num{74.37}. The loss is therefore
strongly non-linear in the first few percent of contact area, as expected when a
small fraction of the surface provides a fast escape path for the trapped mode
volume.

The nominal value is chosen from the crossings themselves, listed in
Table~\ref{tab:contactcross}. At the centre the crossing is remarkably stable
across layouts, $f=\num{0.0165}$--$\num{0.0174}$ for configs~1--3. At the edge
and corner the required fraction drops to \num{0.0033}--\num{0.0088} in the
four-sided layouts, where the diffuse TiO$_2$ reference is bright, while in
the single-sided config~3 it rises to \num{0.0389} (edge) and \num{0.0412}
(corner), because the TiO$_2$ edge and corner patches there are the dimmest of
the three positions. No single $f$ matches every cell exactly; the production
nominal $f=\num{0.045}$ is the smallest sampled fraction that places all nine
cells below TiO$_2$, at the cost of over-suppressing the four-sided centre. At
that value the nine bare yields are
\num{74.37}/\num{64.42}/\num{63.25} (cfg~1),
\num{37.60}/\num{32.35}/\num{32.04} (cfg~2) and
\num{19.22}/\num{16.32}/\num{16.04} (cfg~3) for centre/edge/corner.
The same global $f$ thus matches the ordering in every layout and position; we
retain $f=0$ as the clean-air reference and lower bound on the loss.

\subsection{Aluminium backing: grease versus air}
\label{sec:albacking}

The aluminium foil is the one wrapper whose optical contact is explicitly
ambiguous in the reference construction: grease is applied in places, but
wrinkles and trapped air leave other regions decoupled. The production model
uses the \texttt{air} backing ($n=1.0003$, critical angle
\SI{39.3}{\degree} at the scintillator), which leaves the full TIR cone intact,
so only the sub-critical fraction reaches the foil. The alternative
\texttt{grease} backing ($n=1.46$, critical angle \SI{67.5}{\degree}) lets most
trapped light refract into the grease and return specularly from the foil; it is
retained as the lower-bound systematic. Both use the same tabulated aluminium
reflectance ($R=0.904$--$0.925$). The backing scan covers configs~1--3 at the
three positions, with \num{1000} events per case, using
\texttt{/dcs-monitor/det/muonScint/setAlBacking air|grease}. It is taken from
the earlier complete production campaign because the Al model is unchanged; the
v6 matrix air-backed centre value (\num{479.89}~pe/event) agrees with the v5
scan value (\num{480.05}) to better than \SI{0.1}{\percent}.

Figure~\ref{fig:albacking} and Table~\ref{tab:albacking} show the result. Air
backing raises the aluminium yield by a factor \num{2.38}--\num{2.59} depending
on layout and position. At the config-1 centre the foil goes from \num{201.92}
to \num{480.05}~pe/event, at the config-2 centre from \num{102.51} to
\num{254.57}, and at the config-3 centre from \num{51.98} to \num{127.90}. The
air-backed foil reaches \SI{92.2}{\percent}, \SI{93.4}{\percent} and
\SI{91.6}{\percent} of the glossy yield from the production matrix
(Sec.~\ref{sec:yield}) in those three cases, i.e.\ it recovers
the measured near-equality of aluminium and glossy paper. The grease-backed
branch is roughly a factor \num{2.4} lower---about \SI{40}{\percent} of the
glossy yield---and would put the foil below the clean-air ($f=0$) bare plate, so
the backing choice is the dominant Al systematic. The
enhancement is slightly larger at the edge and corner than at the centre for
config~3, but the position dependence within each backing remains weak.

\section{Light collection: yield and the wrapper ordering}
\label{sec:yield}
% =====================================================================

With the surface model fixed at $f=\num{0.045}$ and air-backed aluminium, we now
present the production results.

\subsection{The three-patch matrix scan}

Figure~\ref{fig:yield} shows the per-event optical-photon yield (the
denominator of Eq.~\ref{eq:eff}) for the four reflectors. The mean yield is
essentially independent of the reflector---about \num{19600} photons per event---which is the correct physical behaviour: the reflector changes how
photons are transported, not how many are produced. The small spread reflects
event-to-event variation in the muon path length.

Figure~\ref{fig:detected} shows the corresponding photoelectron spectra. Here
the reflector has a clear effect: at the config-1 centre the calibrated model
gives \num{75.1}\,\pe/event for the contact-limited bare plate,
\num{124.1}\,\pe/event for TiO$_2$, \num{479.9}\,\pe/event for air-backed
aluminium and \num{518.0}\,\pe/event for glossy paper. The ordering is
$\text{bare}<\text{TiO}_2<\text{Al}<\text{glossy}$, with the air-backed foil
close to glossy paper. The bare curve is therefore a broad, low-occupancy
distribution rather than the near-lossless TIR peak obtained at $f=0$; the
difference is the contact-loss systematic of Sec.~\ref{sec:contact}.

The efficiency scale is set by the SiPM PDE and the coupling: of order
\num{19600} photons are produced and the best wrapper delivers
$\approx 519$ photoelectrons, i.e.\ $\approx 2.65\%$. Given a PDE of
$\sim\!0.4$ and the geometric acceptance of a side-mounted sensor, this is
consistent.

The three irradiation patches separate the response by position, and the
tempting single-coordinate summary is the distance $r$ of the patch
from the plate centre, but $r$ is blind to azimuth, and the shortcoming is
decisive here: configs~3 and~4 instrument a \emph{single} face, so the collection
depends on the \emph{direction} from which the plate is irradiated, not merely on
the distance. All three patches also lie on the half of the plate opposite the
instrumented face of the single-sided layouts, so their distances are not even
monotonic in the proximity to a sensor. We therefore keep the three patches
separate and compare them directly.

Figure~\ref{fig:poslayouts} groups the three patches by layout (one panel per
config, four reflector curves) and Fig.~\ref{fig:posrefl} groups them by
reflector (one panel per wrapper, four layout curves). The conspicuous feature
is that the specular wrappers are nearly \emph{flat} across the three patches:
across all four layouts the maximum/minimum patch ratio spans \SIrange{1.5}{3.4}{\percent}
for aluminium and \SIrange{0.8}{3.2}{\percent} for glossy paper. The diffuse
TiO$_2$ paint is the opposite extreme, varying by \SIrange{74.8}{87.4}{\percent}
(a factor \num{1.75}--\num{1.87}). The contact-limited bare plate lies between
them: \SIrange{4.7}{18.9}{\percent}, with the largest spread in the
four-sided layouts.

The \emph{sign} of the position trend is wrapper- and layout-dependent, and
this is the first place where the simulation and the measurement part company.
For the bare plate, aluminium and glossy paper the three-patch ordering is
centre $>$ edge $>$ corner in every layout, i.e.\ the corner is the dimmest
patch, exactly as reported in Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}. For the diffuse TiO$_2$
paint the trend is \emph{reversed} in the four-sided layouts~1 and~2
(corner $>$ edge $>$ centre, a factor $\approx\!\num{1.75}$), while in the
single-sided layouts~3 and~4 the centre is again the brightest patch. The
mechanism behind this reversal is discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanism}; it is
the same mechanism that sets the uniformity of the full-plate maps and the
ability of the SiPM pattern to localise the muon.

\subsection{The whole-plate average}

The matrix scan pools only three \SI{50}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{50}{\milli\meter}
patches, all on the $-y$ half of the plate, so the efficiency it yields depends
on the choice of patches and is \emph{not} a plate average. The whole-slab run
removes that bias: the GPS plane is expanded to
$250 \times \SI{250}{\milli\meter}$ (\texttt{halfx} $=$ \texttt{halfy} $=$

(config, reflector), so the primary muon's first-entry position is sampled
\emph{uniformly} over the entire plate and the pooled ratio
$\sum\pe/\sum\ngam$ is the \emph{plate-averaged} collection efficiency---the
appropriate global figure of merit. Figure~\ref{fig:bars} shows it for every
(config, reflector) pair, and Table~\ref{tab:eff} lists the numbers.

\[
  \text{bare} < \text{TiO}_2 < \text{Al} < \text{glossy}
  \quad(\text{configs 1--3}),
\]

\[
  \text{TiO}_2 < \text{bare} < \text{Al} < \text{glossy}

\subsection{Survival versus mixing: the two axes that organise every result}
\label{sec:mechanism}

The ordering of Table~\ref{tab:eff} is not a single effect, and it is worth
separating it into two, because they pull in opposite directions and because
almost every other result in this report follows from the same pair.

\emph{Survival} is the fraction of the trapped light that survives the
transport. It is governed by the critical angle at the plate's non-instrumented
faces and by what happens to the sub-critical fraction that leaves the guided
mode:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the bare plate keeps the full TIR cone, but the sub-critical
        \SI{39.3}{\degree} cone that crosses the air gap reaches the black
        holder and is absorbed---so the bare plate keeps the cone but loses
        everything outside it;
  \item the diffuse paint destroys the cone altogether: there is no TIR at a
        \texttt{groundfrontpainted} surface, and the light is re-emitted
        isotropically, so most of it is re-absorbed on the next encounter;
  \item the two mirrors keep the cone and return the sub-critical fraction
        with a reflectivity of \num{0.90}--\num{0.96}, which is why they are
        the brightest.
\end{itemize}
The bare plate is thus \emph{not} the brightest in spite of being the best
guide, because the region outside the cone is exactly where a black holder
catches everything. Adding the contact loss of Sec.~\ref{sec:contact}---a loss
on \emph{every} bounce, not only on the sub-critical cone---takes it from
second-brightest to weakest, which is what the measurement requires.

\emph{Mixing} is the degree to which the light that survives has forgotten
where it was produced. A cavity bounded by a specular mirror and a TIR guide
is a good mixer; a cavity whose walls are Lambertian is a poor one, because
each re-emission randomises the direction and the light leaves towards
whichever wall is nearest, so the probability of reaching a sensor depends on
the geometry of the plate and on the sensor positions. The three-patch data of
Sec.~\ref{sec:yield} already shows the two extremes: the specular wrappers are
flat to within \SIrange{0.8}{3.4}{\percent} across the three patches, while
the diffuse paint varies by a factor \num{1.75}--\num{1.87}.

The useful statement, and the one that organises the rest of the report, is
that these two axes are \emph{not} independent. The wrappers that survive
well (glossy, Al) mix well, because both properties come from the same
specular+TIR boundary; the wrapper that mixes badly (the paint) survives
badly, for the same reason. The bare plate is the interesting case: it mixes
\emph{excellently} (it is a perfect TIR guide, like the two mirrors) but
survives badly (absorbing wall, plus the contact loss), and it is the only
wrapper for which the two axes disagree. This is visible in the data as a bare plate that is
dim, yet whose response still varies with position by a few to
\SI{19}{\percent}---the residual structure of a cavity that mixes well but
not perfectly.

The consequence for the position reconstruction of Sec.~\ref{sec:recon} is
direct: light sharing carries position information exactly when the mixing is
poor, so the diffuse paint---the dimmest of the reflective wrappers and the
least uniform---is the only one whose \emph{integrated charges} localise the
muon, while the bright, perfectly mixing specular wrappers have to be read out
in time instead.

\begin{equation}

\subsection{A single number for the spread: $\kappa_{\rm 2D}$}

A purely radial contrast is deliberately \emph{not} used.  The read-out layouts
have no continuous rotational symmetry---configs~1 and~2 are four-fold
symmetric, configs~3 and~4 single-sided---and the plate is square, so the
collection efficiency is not a function of radius alone.  Beyond the inscribed
circle ($r > \SI{125}{\milli\meter}$) only the four corner wedges of the plate
are populated, so a fixed-width radial bin would mix a shrinking range of
directions and its radial profile $\varepsilon(r)$ would reflect the geometry of
the square rather than the detector.  The uniformity is therefore quantified
directly in the plate plane $(x,y)$.

The \emph{two-dimensional contrast}
\begin{equation}
  \kappa_{\rm 2D} \;=\; \frac{\max_{\rm cell}\varepsilon}{\min_{\rm cell}\varepsilon}
\end{equation}
collapses the field to a single number, the ratio of the brightest to the
darkest region; it answers ``how large is the spread'' and is the quantity that
ranks the layouts and reflectors.  $\kappa_{\rm 2D}$ is evaluated on a coarser
$5\times5$ grid of \SI{50}{\milli\meter} cells, because on the
\SI{5}{\milli\meter} cells of the maps the per-cell statistics are too thin for
a stable max/min---Poisson noise in the extreme cells would inflate it.  The
measure is therefore deliberately the \emph{robust} dynamic range, and is
smaller than the raw peak-to-trough range of the fine maps.  No symmetry is
assumed, so
$\kappa_{\rm 2D}$ is meaningful for the single-sided layouts~3 and~4 as well as
for the four-sided ones.

The direction-sensitive contrast $\kappa_{\rm 2D}$ ranks the reflectors
consistently in \emph{every} layout,
\[
\text{TiO}_2 \;>\; \text{bare} \;>\; \text{Al} \;\gtrsim\; \text{glossy},
\]
i.e.\ the diffuse TiO$_2$ paint is the \emph{least} uniform wrapper and glossy
paper the most uniform. The diffuse paint is also the dimmest direct-coupled
wrapper, while the bright specular wrappers are the most uniform: the specular
transport mixes the light so thoroughly that the entry position is largely
erased ($\kappa_{\rm 2D}\approx1.1$--\num{1.3}), whereas the diffuse paint
retains a pronounced positional response. The contact-limited bare plate sits
between them because the holder loss is itself position dependent.
Numerically the TiO$_2$ contrast grows from $\kappa_{\rm 2D} = \num{1.911}$ and \num{2.579}
in the four-sided layouts~1 and~2 to \num{8.146} and \num{8.311} in the
single-sided layouts~3 and~4; the bare plate spans
\num{1.36}--\num{3.82}, aluminium \num{1.08}--\num{1.28} and glossy
\num{1.07}--\num{1.28}. A muon crossing anywhere on the plate is therefore
collected within about \SI{30}{\percent} of the most favourable point for
Al/glossy, within a factor of \num{3.8} for the contact-limited bare plate, and
within a factor of \num{8.3} for TiO$_2$ in the single-sided layouts.

\subsection{The shape of the field: distance to the nearest sensor}
\label{sec:nearest}

The contrast number of the previous subsection says how large the spread is,
but not what shape it has, and the maps show that the shape is not radial at
all. For config~1 the bare plate maps to a \emph{checkerboard}: the
$50\times\SI{50}{\milli\meter}$ cell efficiencies alternate between
$3.3\times10^{-3}$ and $5.1\times10^{-3}$ from cell to cell, with the bright
cells being exactly those that contain a SiPM. For config~2 the bare plate
shows the same thing more sharply: the four cells that contain the four sensors
read $3.10$--$3.35\times10^{-3}$ while the remaining twenty-one read
$1.63$--$2.08\times10^{-3}$. For config~3 the bare plate has two bright cells
($2.13\times10^{-3}$ and $2.10\times10^{-3}$) at the two sensor positions, but
the cell \emph{between} them---on the instrumented face, but not on a sensor---
is the darkest of the whole plate ($0.56\times10^{-3}$): light produced there
leaves through the uninstrumented part of that face before it can reach a
sensor. The diffuse TiO$_2$ paint shows
the complementary structure: a bright frame, with the cells adjacent to the
sensors reaching $1.21\times10^{-2}$ and the central cross falling to
$6.4$--$8.0\times10^{-3}$.

These patterns are all produced by the same rule, and the rule has a single
coordinate: \emph{the response tracks, on average, the distance from the
interaction point to the nearest collection site}. Where the cavity mixes the
light well, the response is independent of that distance; where it mixes
poorly, the response falls off as the interaction point moves away from the
sensors, because the light then has a longer and more lossy path to reach one.
The rule is a statement about the \emph{envelope} of the map and not a
point-by-point description of it: the cell between the two sensors of config~3
quoted above is a counterexample to a strict monotonicity, and the reason is
visible---that cell lies on the instrumented face, so its light leaves the
plate immediately through the part of that face that carries no sensor. The
distance statistic below integrates over such configurations, so what it
measures is the trend that survives the averaging.

Figure~\ref{fig:nearest} tests this directly, by binning the whole-slab
events in the distance $d$ to the nearest collection site and plotting the
efficiency normalised to the plate average against it. For configs~1--3 the
collection sites are the SiPM tiles; for config~4 the collection aperture is
the fibre clamp, a line along the top edge, so $d$ is the distance to that
line. The four curves of each panel share one shape but not one level:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the two specular wrappers are flat to within \SI{5}{\percent} over
        the whole range, in every layout---they are near-perfect light mixers;
  \item the contact-limited bare plate shows a clear excess in the first
        \SI{25}{\milli\meter} ($1.45\times$, $2.07\times$ and
        $3.03\times$ the plate average for configs~1--3) and then relaxes to
        slightly \emph{below} unity, which is the signature of a cavity that
        mixes well but loses a little light on every extra bounce;
  \item the diffuse paint shows the largest excess ($1.63\times$,
        $2.37\times$ and $4.43\times$) and the steepest decay, reaching
        $0.37$--$0.68$ of the average at the far side of the plate in the
        single-sided layouts.
\end{itemize}
This single figure therefore contains the explanation of the whole uniformity
result: the specular wrappers are uniform because they mix, the paint is
non-uniform because it does not, and the bare plate sits in between because
the contact loss destroys the perfect mixing of the air-gap guide without
destroying its geometry. It also explains the sign flip of the position trend
seen in the three-patch scan: in the four-sided layouts the corner patch is
close to two sensors and is therefore the brightest, while in the single-sided
layouts the sensors are on the far side of the plate and the centre of the
plate---the point furthest from any sensor---is not the dimmest point, because
what matters is the distance to the \emph{nearest} sensor, and the single
sensor still beats the far corners.

\section{Position reconstruction from the SiPM pattern}
\label{sec:recon}
% =====================================================================

The eight SiPMs of config~1 view the same scintillation event from eight
directions.  A natural question for a large-area muon trigger built this way is
whether the \emph{relative} pattern of the eight channels---their integrated
charges, their relative arrival times, and the prompt fraction of their
charge---carries enough information to localise the interaction point inside
the plate.  The whole-plate irradiation of Sec.~\ref{sec:yield} is the data set
that answers it: it samples the interaction point uniformly over the
\SI{250}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{250}{\milli\meter} footprint and records, for
every event, both the ground truth (the muon's first-entry position) and the
complete per-SiPM response.

\subsection{The observables and the estimator}
\label{sec:recon-method}

For every event the \texttt{sipmHits} tree carries, per SiPM, the number of
detected photoelectrons $N_s$, the time of the first detected photoelectron
$t^{\mathrm{first}}_s$, its mean arrival time $\bar t_s$, the number of
photoelectrons that arrived within \SI{3}{\nano\second} of the interaction
$N^{\mathrm{prompt}}_s$ (the prompt fraction), and the mean detected
wavelength.  These are exactly the quantities a read-out chain provides: an
integrated charge, a threshold-crossing or time-of-flight, and a pulse shape.

It is worth being explicit about what $N_s$ is, because the distinction
matters for the electronics.  $N_s$ counts \emph{every} photoelectron detected
in the channel over the whole event, so it is the \emph{integral} of the light
pulse delivered to that SiPM (multiplied by the gain), not the amplitude of the
pulse peak.  This is the natural choice: the integral carries the full
Poisson statistics of the collected charge, whereas a peak-amplitude
measurement uses only the prompt subset of the light and is additionally
biased low by the spread of the photoelectron arrival times.  In the present
data the prompt fraction (photoelectrons arriving before \SI{3}{\nano\second})
is \SI{53}{\percent} of the light for the bare plate, \SI{34}{\percent} for
the TiO$_2$ paint and only \SIrange{15}{16}{\percent} for the two specular
wrappers, so a peak-amplitude read-out would work with a small minority of the
collected photons.  No peak-amplitude observable is used anywhere in this
section; the position information is extracted from pulse integrals and from
arrival times.

Only the transverse position can be reconstructed: the muon is vertical and
traverses the whole \SI{10}{\milli\meter} thickness, so the light is produced
along a vertical track and the side pattern carries no usable $z$ information
(the corresponding path-length difference between the two faces of the plate is
\SI{10}{\milli\meter}, i.e.\ \SI{0.05}{\nano\second}, an order of magnitude
below the timing signals below).

Two families of estimator are evaluated on the same data.  The first is
transparent and purely analytic: the \emph{opposite-pair} observables
\begin{equation}
  A_x = \frac{N_{\mathrm{right}}-N_{\mathrm{left}}}
             {N_{\mathrm{right}}+N_{\mathrm{left}}},
  \qquad
  T_x = \bar t_{\mathrm{right}}-\bar t_{\mathrm{left}},
  \label{eq:pair}
\end{equation}
formed from the two SiPMs on the right and the two on the left edge (and the
analogous $A_y$, $T_y$ for the top and bottom edges).  $N_{\mathrm{right}}$ and
$N_{\mathrm{left}}$ are the summed pulse integrals of the two channels of the
corresponding edge, so $A_x$ is a pure charge asymmetry: it is the measured
light sharing, free of the total yield.  Both observables are odd functions of
the corresponding coordinate, so a single linear fit of $A_x$ against $x$
measures the response directly, and the resolution that this read-out could
achieve follows from the noise on the fit.

The second family is a \emph{learned response function}: the reconstructed
position is the mean position of the $k$ training events whose eight-channel
pattern is closest to the pattern of the event being reconstructed
($k$-nearest-neighbour regression in the standardised feature space, with
$k=40$).  This is the simulation stand-in for the response function that a
real experiment would obtain from a calibration scan, and it makes no
assumption about the form of the response---which matters, because the
timing channel turns out to carry the position information in a strongly
\emph{non-linear} way.  Of the \num{25000} events of each wrapper,
\num{20000} are used for training and \num{5000} are held out; the quoted
resolutions are the rms of the reconstruction error on the held-out set.

\label{sec:recon-pattern}

Figure~\ref{fig:recon-wf} shows the per-SiPM arrival-time waveforms for two

For the central muon the eight waveforms are indistinguishable: every SiPM

specular glossy paper the light is mixed so thoroughly by the
total-internal-reflection guide that the light share changes by at most
\SI{1.4}{\percent}---there is essentially \emph{no} light sharing---but the
arrival times still separate: the two corner sensors fire about
\SI{0.8}{\nano\second} earlier than the sensors on the opposite edges.

\subsection{A single linear combination is not enough}
\label{sec:recon-linear}

Table~\ref{tab:reconobs} quantifies the linear read-out of Eq.~\ref{eq:pair}.
For the TiO$_2$ paint the charge asymmetry is a clean, first-order position
observable: $A_x$ changes by \num{7.4e-3} per millimetre, so that the
\SI{18.2}{\milli\meter} rms noise of $A_x$ corresponds to a position
uncertainty of \SI{18.2}{\milli\meter} along $x$ from a \emph{single}
combination of two pairs of sensors.  For the two specular wrappers the same
slope is \num{5e-5}, i.e.\ two orders of magnitude smaller: a TIR light guide
mixes the light so effectively that the light distribution carries no
first-order information about where the light was injected.  The arrival-time
difference is informative for TiO$_2$ (a slope of \num{-17.3}\,\si{\ps\per\mm},
i.e.\ the nearer edge fires earlier) but its noise is an order of magnitude
larger, and for the glossy and Al wrappers the timing slope is
\SI{1.8}{\ps\per\mm} against a noise of \SI{0.46}{\nano\second}---a
\SI{250}{\milli\meter} uncertainty, i.e.\ no information at all.  The bare
plate sits in between: its holder losses are themselves position dependent, but
weakly and with the opposite sign in the timing channel.

The answer to the question is therefore \emph{yes}, with an important
qualification about \emph{which} information is used.  With the diffuse
TiO$_2$ paint the light sharing alone localises the muon to
\SI{12.5}{\milli\meter} per axis (\SI{17.5}{\milli\meter} radially, i.e.\
\SI{5}{\percent} of the plate width), and the timing adds nothing.  With the
two specular wrappers the situation is exactly reversed: the integrated charges
are useless (\num{44}--\SI{45}{\milli\meter}, little better than a random guess
over the plate), and the arrival-time pattern alone reaches
\SI{19}{\milli\meter} per axis.  A TIR light guide is a very good light
mixer, so the charge pattern carries no position information, but the
\emph{time} at which the first light reaches each edge still does, because the
nearby sensors receive the prompt component earlier.  The bare plate, whose
holder losses are position dependent without being either first order or
strong, sits in between at \SI{22}{\milli\meter}.

This is the same two-axis structure as Sec.~\ref{sec:mechanism}: the wrapper
that mixes badly (the paint) is the one whose \emph{integrated charges} localise
the muon, and the wrappers that mix well (the mirrors) are the ones that have to
be read out in time. The gain in resolution is bought with a factor of seven
in light yield, so the choice of wrapper is a choice between photon economy
and position information, not an optimisation of either alone.

\subsection{Response and resolution map}

Figure~\ref{fig:recon-resp} shows the response of the estimator: the mean
reconstructed position in \SI{25}{\milli\meter} bins of the true position,
with the rms error of each bin as a vertical bar, against the one-to-one line.

example).  The second is the growing error bar towards the plate edges, where
the local density of the pattern space is lower.  Both are properties of the

remove the shrinkage.

\subsection{What this would require in practice}
\label{sec:recon-practice}

The quoted resolutions are those of an idealised read-out, and the
qualifications are worth stating explicitly.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Which electronics are needed depends on the wrapper.}  With a
        diffuse reflector the position comes from the light sharing, so the
        integrated charges of the eight channels suffice and no fast timing
        electronics are required: \SI{12.5}{\milli\meter} per axis.  With a
        specular wrapper the integrated charges are worthless and the position comes
        from the arrival times, which requires a per-channel time
        digitisation with a resolution well below the \SI{0.5}{\nano\second}
        separations seen in Fig.~\ref{fig:recon-obs}.  A TDC of
        \SI{100}{\ps} resolution is comfortably sufficient for the
        \SI{19}{\milli\meter} quoted here.
  \item \textbf{The charge observable is the pulse integral, not the pulse
        amplitude.}  The numbers above are obtained from the integrated
        charge of the whole light pulse, which is what a charge-integrating
        read-out chain (a slow shaper or a charge-sensitive ADC over the
        full pulse) delivers.  A read-out that instead recorded only the
        \emph{peak} of the shaped pulse would work with the prompt subset of
        the light---\SI{53}{\percent} of it for the bare plate, \SI{34}{\percent}
        for the TiO$_2$ paint and \SIrange{15}{16}{\percent} for the two
        specular wrappers---and would lose statistical information for the
        same reason that a peak statistic is always noisier than an
        integral.  The simulation contains no shaping, so the effect is not
        quantified here; it is a systematic of the read-out, not of the
        detector.
  \item \textbf{The timing observable used here is optimistic.}  The
        \emph{first arrival} is the earliest of the 10--65 photoelectrons
        detected in a channel, i.e.\ a biased order statistic whose statistical
        error is large; a real read-out would deliver a threshold-crossing
        time or a pulse time-of-flight instead.  Both were tried during the
        analysis of this section; the mean arrival time of the prompt
        component behaves similarly.
  \item \textbf{The simulation contains no electronics.}  There is no SiPM
        saturation, no electronic noise, no threshold, no gain spread, no dark
        counts and no time jitter, and the PDE is applied exactly as
        tabulated.  Every one of these widens the pattern distributions and
        therefore degrades the resolution quoted above; the numbers should be
        read as the resolution of the \emph{optical} read-out, i.e.\ an upper
        bound for a real device.
  \item \textbf{The response function is learned from simulation.}  In a real
        experiment the equivalent information would come from a calibration
        scan of the plate (the same scan that produces the collection-efficiency
        maps of Figs.~\ref{fig:wholeslab}--\ref{fig:wholeslab4}), and the
        estimator would be a fit to that measured response function rather
        than a nearest-neighbour search in simulated events.
  \item \textbf{Only the transverse position is accessible.}  A vertical
        muon traverses the full thickness, so the pattern carries no usable
        information about the depth of the interaction; a tilted or
        oblique beam would still localise the transverse position, but the
        depth would remain unconstrained.
\end{itemize}

The practical conclusion is that a side-read-out muon trigger of this
geometry can be turned into a coarse position detector---at the
\SI{12}--\SI{20}{\milli\meter} level, i.e.\ a discretisation of the plate into
roughly ten cells per axis---without any change to the optical design, but
only if the wrapper is chosen with the read-out chain in mind: a diffuse
reflector for a charge-only read-out, or a fast TDC if the plate is wrapped
in a specular mirror.

\subsection{The bare configuration and the detection-efficiency threshold}

The statement about the \SI{80}{\percent} threshold is the one that can be
made quantitative without electronics, because the threshold enters the
detection efficiency only through the requirement that an event deposit more
than a certain number of photoelectrons. Writing $N_{\rm thr}$ for that
number, the simulated detection efficiency of Eq.~\ref{eq:de} is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{DE}(N_{\rm thr}) = P\!\left(\sum_s N_s \;\ge\; N_{\rm thr}\right),
  \label{eq:de-sim}
\end{equation}
and the absolute value of $N_{\rm thr}$ corresponding to the experiment's
$400\,\mathrm{mV}$ is not known (it depends on the SiPM gain, the electronics
gain and the threshold setting). The statement itself, however, is
\emph{threshold-free}: it says that there \emph{exists} a working threshold at
which the three reflective treatments are above \SI{80}{\percent} and the bare
one is not.

Figure~\ref{fig:de} shows the simulated curves of Eq.~\ref{eq:de-sim} for the
four treatments of each layout, which have exactly the shape of the measured
detection-efficiency curves of Fig.~11 of Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}: the bare
plate's curve is shifted far to the left, and the three reflective curves are
nearly on top of one another. Table~\ref{tab:compareDE} lists, for each
layout, the window of thresholds in which the experiment's statement holds.
In the three directly-coupled layouts the window is non-empty and wide
($57$--$130$ photoelectrons for config~1), and it is empty only for config~4,
where the bare plate is not the weakest wrapper at all and where the
experiment reports no bare measurement.

\subsection{The one disagreement: the position trend of the diffuse paint}

For three of the four treatments the simulation reproduces the measured sign of
the position trend: the corner of the plate is the dimmest place, as reported
in Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}. The diffuse TiO$_2$ paint is the exception. In the
four-sided layouts~1 and~2 the corner patch is the \emph{brightest} of the
three, a factor \num{1.75} above the centre, and in the single-sided
layouts~3 and~4 the centre is the brightest and the corner the dimmest, which
is the measured sign.

The mechanism is the one of Sec.~\ref{sec:nearest}. A Lambertian paint is a
poor light mixer, so the response tracks the distance to the nearest sensor.
In a four-sided layout the corner patch is close to \emph{two} sensors and is
therefore the brightest, while the centre of the plate is the furthest point
from any of the eight sensors and is the dimmest---which is what the
three-patch scan of Table~\ref{tab:positions} shows
($6.380\times10^{-3}$ at the centre against $1.121\times10^{-2}$ at the
corner). In a single-sided layout the same rule applies with a single
collection site, and there the centre of the plate is closer to the sensor
than the far corners are, so the sign of the trend is reversed.

The same rule explains why the paint is the *least* uniform wrapper
($\kappa_{\rm 2D} = \num{1.91}$--\num{8.31}) while the specular wrappers are
nearly flat, and why the paint is the only wrapper whose light sharing
carries a first-order position signal. The disagreement with the measurement
is therefore not a random discrepancy: it is a single, identified difference in
how strongly the light is mixed, and it is confined to one treatment. The
experiment's own report does not state which of the two competing effects
dominates in the paint, so the discrepancy cannot be attributed from the
published data alone; in the model, the sign is set by the number of sensors
on the illuminated side of the plate.

\section{Caveats and open items}
\label{sec:caveats}

\paragraph{Config 4 collects less light than config 3, the opposite of the
measurement.}
With the nominal surfaces, config~4 reaches \num{67.5}~\pe/event against
\num{140.2}~\pe/event for config~3 in the glossy treatment, a factor \num{2.1}
too few, whereas Fig.~12 of Ref.~\cite{Rao2025} shows config~4 producing a
significantly \emph{higher} pulse height than config~3. The experiment reports
that the WLS-converted photons produce electrical signals with approximately
\SI{30}{\percent} efficiency, so a substantial loss in the wavelength-shifting
path is expected and is not reproduced here. The geometric cause is visible in
the maps: in config~4 the light has to reach a single SiPM through four
\SI{1.0}{\milli\meter} fibres, of which only the converged bundle is actually
read out, and the surviving light is then transported along the fibre to the
read-out at one end. The simulation does model the fibre, the multi-clad
index staircase, the far-end mirrors, the exponential re-emission and the
photocathode, but it does not model the finite acceptance of the fibre mouths
or the collection inefficiency of the re-emission at the fibre end, both of
which are candidate causes. A quantitative light budget---photons produced,
coupled into the fibres, surviving the transport, re-emitted within the
acquisition window, and detected---has not been made, and this is the largest
open item of the study. The comparison also inherits the caveat that the
experiment itself states, namely that the four configurations cannot be
compared directly because the number of parallel SiPMs changes the gain, the
input capacitance and the effective photon-coupling probability; the
experiment's config-4 value is a pulse height over a \SI{2500}{\milli\meter\squared}
fibre-augmented effective area, not a raw photoelectron count.

\paragraph{Two of the three reflector reflectances are fitted, not measured.}
Only the aluminium reflectance is taken from data (the evaporated-film
normal-incidence reflectance of McPeak \emph{et al.}~\cite{McPeak2015}). The
TiO$_2$ paint curve is a representative white-paint curve
($R \approx 0.955$--$0.983$) and the glossy paper curve
($R \approx 0.900$--$0.955$) is fitted to the statement that the two best
wrappers give nearly equal signals. The robustness of the reported
\emph{ordering} to a systematic shift of these curves has not been tested; the
ordering is expected to be robust, because the TiO$_2$ paint is separated from
the mirrors by the absence of TIR rather than by a difference in reflectance,
but this remains an untested assumption.

\paragraph{The WLS absorption length is a systematic band, not a scanned
parameter.}
The absolute strength of the Y-11 absorption is set by the absorption length
at \SI{430}{\nano\meter}, taken as \SI{1.0}{\milli\meter} with a plausible band
of \SIrange{0.3}{3}{\milli\meter}. The config-4 yield is directly sensitive to
it, and the band has not been scanned. The quoted config-4 numbers should
therefore be read with a systematic band of a few tens of per cent on the
yield.

\paragraph{Bare-holder contact is effective, not measured.}
Modelled with a real \SI{1.2}{\milli\meter} air gap between the polished plate
and the black holder ($f=0$), the bare configuration is a near-lossless TIR
light guide and is the \emph{second}-brightest wrapper in every layout, not the
weakest. Reproducing the experimental suppression requires the plate--holder
interface to be optically \emph{lossy}, i.e.\ partial real contact (frustrated
TIR). The extended contact scan shows that the crossing fraction is
$\approx\num{0.017}$ at the centre in every layout, \num{0.003}--\num{0.009} at
the edge/corner in configs~1--2, and \num{0.039}--\num{0.041} at the edge/corner
of config~3. No single $f$ matches every cell exactly; the production nominal
$f=\num{0.045}$ is the smallest sampled fraction that places all nine cells
below TiO$_2$. The fraction is an effective area fraction, not a measured holder
gap, so $f=0$ is retained as the clean-air reference and lower bound on the
contact loss.

\paragraph{Position trend of the diffuse paint.}
As discussed in Sec.~\ref{sec:comparison}, the sign of the position trend of
the TiO$_2$ paint is layout-dependent in the simulation and disagrees with the
single trend reported in Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}. The mechanism is identified
(the nearest-sensor rule of Sec.~\ref{sec:nearest}), but the simulation has not
been checked against an independent set of simulation settings, and the
experiment does not report the light-mixing behaviour that would decide the
issue.

\paragraph{Position reconstruction is limited by the optics, not by the estimator.}
Sec.~\ref{sec:recon} shows that the relative pattern of the eight SiPM
channels of config~1 does localise the interaction point, to
\SI{12.5}{\milli\meter} per axis with the diffuse TiO$_2$ paint and
\SI{19}{\milli\meter} with the specular wrappers, but the quoted numbers are
those of an \emph{optical} read-out: the simulation contains no SiPM
saturation, no electronic noise, no threshold, no gain spread, no dark counts
and no time jitter.  The timing channel additionally assumes a per-channel
time digitisation well below the \SI{0.5}{\nano\second} separations that carry
the signal, and the response function is learned from simulated events rather
than measured.  The reported resolution should therefore be read as an upper
bound for a real device.

\paragraph{Scale and normalisation.}
The absolute efficiencies are model-dependent: they are fixed by the assumed
PDE curve, the grease coupling, and the absorbed fraction of the tabulated
reflectivities. The SiPM breakdown voltage and gain (which drive the PDE) are
also temperature-dependent~\cite{Anfimov2021}, an effect not modelled here. The
robust results are the \emph{ordering} (glossy paper brightest and most uniform,
air-backed aluminium second, contact-limited bare plate weakest in the
direct-coupled layouts, and TiO$_2$ weakest in the WLS layout), the
threshold statement of Table~\ref{tab:compareDE}, and the full-plate contrast
hierarchy, not the absolute efficiencies.

\paragraph{Optical-track stall (resolved).}
During earlier matrix production runs a rare stall was observed: a single worker
thread would enter a tight loop (100\% CPU) while the remaining threads waited at
the event-level barrier, freezing the run. It was traced to insufficiently
robust photon tracking in the \emph{tessellated} config-4 fibre solid, where
near-coincident facet boundaries could leave a track stuck. The production
config-4 geometry now uses the analytic, mitered \texttt{G4CutTubs} fibre legs
(Sec.~\ref{sec:wls}) instead, whose straight, planar miter interfaces eliminate
that class of degenerate boundary; the stall has not recurred since. As a safety
net the production workflow still wraps every task in a timeout with automatic
retry.

\section{Summary}
\label{sec:summary}

A \gfour\ optical simulation of a
\SI{250}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{250}{\milli\meter}$\times$\SI{10}{\milli\meter}
polished plastic scintillator read out by side-mounted SiPMs has been developed
and applied to a full matrix of four SiPM layouts and four reflector treatments
at three beam positions (\num{120000} events), complemented by a uniform
whole-plate irradiation (\num{400000} events), a \num{99000}-event bare-contact
scan and an \num{18000}-event aluminium-backing scan (\num{637000} events in
total). The optical physics---unified surface model, finish selection,
photocathode PDE via the boundary process, and per-event photon
accounting---has been verified against the \gfour\ source.

The starting point of the study is a paradox: of the four surface treatments
compared in Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}, the bare polished plate is the one that loses
the least light optically---it is a total-internal-reflection light guide
facing a black wall---and yet it is the dimmest in the measurement. Two
unmeasured surface parameters had to be calibrated before the measured
ordering could be reproduced at all: the mean-field plate--holder contact
fraction, locked at $f=\num{0.045}$ as the smallest sampled value that places
all nine direct-coupled cells below TiO$_2$, and the optical backing of the
aluminium foil, taken as air because it reproduces the measured near-equality
of aluminium and glossy paper. With them, the direct-coupled layouts reproduce
the experimentally observed ordering
\[
  \text{bare} < \text{TiO}_2 < \text{Al} < \text{glossy},
\]
glossy paper being the brightest and the most uniform wrapper, air-backed
aluminium within \SI{92}{\percent} of it, and the contact-limited bare plate
the weakest; in the WLS layout the ordering is instead
$\text{TiO}_2<\text{bare}<\text{Al}<\text{glossy}$, because the black holder is
absent there. The measured statement that the bare configuration is the only
one to fail the \SI{80}{\percent} detection-efficiency criterion is reproduced
as well, and is shown to be a statement about the *threshold window* rather
than about a single number.

Underneath the ordering there is a single mechanism, and it organises the rest
of the results. Two axes matter: the \emph{survival} of the trapped light,
which sets the yield, and the \emph{mixing} of that light, which sets the
uniformity of the response and the information content of the sensor pattern.
The specular wrappers win on both; the diffuse paint wins on neither, and
therefore is the dimmest wrapper but the one whose light sharing localises the
muon; the bare plate wins on mixing and loses on survival, and is the only
wrapper for which the two disagree. The unifying coordinate is the distance
from the interaction point to the nearest collection site: the response tracks
it whenever the cavity is not a perfect light mixer, which is why the full-plate
contrast runs from \num{1.07} (glossy) to \num{8.31} (diffuse paint in the
single-sided layout), and why the three-patch position trend of the paint
reverses its sign between the four-sided and the single-sided layouts.

The same mechanism makes the eight channels of config~1 a position detector.
With the diffuse paint the light sharing alone fixes the interaction point to
\SI{12.5}{\milli\meter} per axis (\SI{17.5}{\milli\meter} radially,
\SI{5}{\percent} of the plate width), with no timing electronics at all; the
specular wrappers are useless in pulse integral (\num{44}--\SI{45}{\milli\meter}) but
reach \SI{19}{\milli\meter} per axis through the arrival-time pattern, given a
fast enough TDC. The contact-limited bare plate sits in between at
\SI{22}{\milli\meter}. These are the resolutions of the optical read-out
alone: the simulation contains no SiPM saturation, electronic noise, threshold,
gain spread or time jitter, and a real device would do worse. The WLS
read-out of config~4 is delayed by about \SI{6}{\nano\second} and green-shifted
to \SI{509.5}{\nano\meter} by the Y-11 fibres, confirming that the WLS path is
active; it is also the one configuration whose efficiency is not reproduced,
collecting a factor \num{2.1} less light than config~3 where the measurement
finds more, which remains the largest open item of the study.

Absolute efficiencies remain model-dependent (PDE, coupling, and tabulated
reflectivities); the robust results are the wrapper and layout orderings, the
threshold statement of Table~\ref{tab:compareDE}, the full-plate contrast
hierarchy, and the fact that the position information carried by a
side-read-out trigger depends on the wrapper rather than on the read-out
electronics.

\appendix
\section{\gfour\ optical-physics implementation notes}
\label{app:g4}
% =====================================================================

This appendix collects the verification of the \gfour\ optical boundary
physics that the model relies on. It is reference material rather than a
result, and is kept out of the main text for that reason.

\subsection{The boundary process and the unified surface model}

Photon crossing of a dielectric interface is handled by
\texttt{G4OpBoundaryProcess}, which selects an \texttt{G4OpticalSurface} from the
unified surface model of Levin and Moisan~\cite{Levin1996} (whose key
parameters for Geant4/GATE photon transport were later
characterised~\cite{Nilsson2015}), using
(\emph{i}) a \texttt{G4LogicalBorderSurface} between two physical volumes or
(\emph{ii}) a \texttt{G4LogicalSkinSurface} on a logical volume. Reading the
source (lines 319--347), the lookup is: a border surface between the pre- and
post-step volumes is consulted first; if none exists, the skin surface of the
daughter (or, failing that, the mother) logical volume is used. This establishes
two facts on which the geometry relies:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item a \texttt{G4LogicalBorderSurface} takes precedence over a
        \texttt{G4LogicalSkinSurface} at the same boundary---hence the grease
        and SiPM patches punch ``holes'' in the wrapping skin;
  \item if no surface object exists at a boundary, the process falls back to
        the default lossless TIR behaviour (via \texttt{DielectricDielectric()}).
\end{enumerate}

The boundary process requires a valid \texttt{RINDEX}: on medium~1 always, and
on medium~2 for a \texttt{polished}/\texttt{ground}
\texttt{dielectric\_dielectric} boundary (lines 289--306, 436--453);
\texttt{*backpainted} finishes instead read the backing index from the
\emph{surface} MPT (lines 356--374). If the property is absent the process sets
status \texttt{NoRINDEX} and kills the photon \emph{silently}---the message is
printed only above verbose level~1---which is why every material (and every
backpainted surface) in the model carries an explicit \texttt{RINDEX} table.
This silent kill is also why the far-end fibre mirror needed an explicit skin
(Sec.~\ref{sec:wls}): the metal cap has no \texttt{RINDEX}, so the un-covered
core-to-cap crossing was being lost.

For the \emph{unified} model the probability weights for the reflection
sub-models are read from the surface MPT---\texttt{SPECULARSPIKECONSTANT}
($f_{ss}$), \texttt{SPECULARLOBECONSTANT} ($f_{sl}$) and
\texttt{BACKSCATTERCONSTANT} ($f_{bs}$)---together with \texttt{REFLECTIVITY}
(line 381) and, where applicable, \texttt{TRANSMITTANCE} (line 394) and
\texttt{EFFICIENCY} (line 390). The surviving probability
$1 - f_{ss} - f_{sl} - f_{bs}$ is shared between diffuse (Lambertian) and bare
reflection.

\subsection{Reflector finishes}

The four treatments use three distinct code paths in
\texttt{G4OpBoundaryProcess}, which we verified:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{\texttt{groundfrontpainted}} (TiO$_2$). The finish is handled by
        the ``painted-front'' branch (lines 462--488): a uniform random draw
        against \texttt{REFLECTIVITY} decides absorbed / transmitted / reflected,
        and reflection is set to a pure Lambertian distribution
        (\texttt{LambertianReflection}, line 481). There is \emph{no} TIR at this
        finish---photons are either diffusely reflected or absorbed. This is the
        physically correct description of sub-surface scattering in a thick
        TiO$_2$ paint.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{polishedbackpainted}} (Al, glossy paper). This finish is
        routed to \texttt{DielectricDielectric()} (line 456--459). The
        scintillator/backing interface still exhibits Fresnel reflection and TIR;
        sub-critical photons cross it and are returned by the backing ``paint''
        with reflectivity $R(\lambda)$ and a reflection distribution set by
        $f_{ss}, f_{sl}, f_{bs}$. We choose $f_{ss} = 1$ (pure specular spike),
        since both wrappers are smooth mirrors, and set the backing refractive
        index to distinguish the two physical situations. For the aluminium foil
        the backing index is the coupling grease ($n = 1.46$), following
        Ref.~\cite{Rao2025}, which applies grease so the foil optically contacts
        the plate; the critical angle is then only
        $\arcsin(1.46/1.58) = \SI{67.5}{\degree}$, so most light refracts out of
        the plate and is returned specularly by the foil, whose reflectivity is
        the tabulated normal-incidence reflectance of evaporated aluminium. For
        glossy paper the backing index is that of air ($n = 1.0003$), so the
        plate keeps its full TIR cone and only the sub-critical fraction reaches
        the paper, which acts as a slightly lossier mirror.
  \item \textbf{Bare plate}. This is deliberately \emph{not} given a wrapping
        skin. The plate keeps its plain polished boundary across a real
        \SI{1.2}{\milli\meter} air gap to the holder shell. The sub-critical
        fraction refracts across that gap and is then killed by the holder: the
        black shell carries an absorbing skin ($R = T = 0$), and its bulk
        (\SI{1}{\micro\meter} absorption length) would absorb the light anyway.
        Super-critical light is retained by TIR. There is \emph{no} absorbing
        skin on the plate itself; the same air-gap loss is applied at the
        read-out pads, which are separated from the plate by a thin air border
        in this configuration.
\end{itemize}
